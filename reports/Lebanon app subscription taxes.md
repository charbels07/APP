# Stores collect foreign VAT; Lebanon's taxes stay yours

Apple and Google calculate, collect and pay the sales taxes (VAT, GST, US sales tax) on your subscriptions in most of the world. Apple does it in the 94 regions listed in its agreement. Google does it in the EU and UK, where it is the legal seller, and in about 50 other countries plus the US states that have a sales tax. **Neither store collects any tax on sales to customers in Lebanon, and neither pays any of your Lebanese taxes.** In Lebanon you owe **income tax** on your profit, because Lebanese law taxes profit from work done in Lebanon even when every buyer and every payer is abroad. For an individual the rate runs from **4% to 25%**, applied after a personal allowance of about **$5,000**. That comes to roughly **$2,000 of tax on $30,000 of profit** and **$14,000 on $100,000**, at the official rate of 89,500 Lebanese pounds (LBP) per dollar. On **VAT (TVA)**, the law requires every "exporter" to register "whatever their turnover", and the only official reading (Ministry of Finance, 2017) applies that to exporters of services. The safe course is therefore to register from the first sale. Your foreign sales are then taxed at **0%**; the one open question is the slice sold to subscribers in Lebanon, which may owe **11%**. Tax registration is due within two months of starting. There is no compulsory social security (NSSF) for a freelancer. Since January 2026 the Ministry has said it will pursue people who are not registered, and the 2026 budget multiplied fixed fines by 25. Outside Lebanon: the US has **no tax treaty with Lebanon**, but a correctly filed W-8BEN form should keep US withholding at zero on ordinary app sales. The real foreign losses are Brazil (where Google withholds an extra 10% because Brazil lists Lebanon as a tax haven), Vietnam and Taiwan. For a small developer, both stores keep about **15%** of each subscription. Google pays Lebanese developers in US dollars by bank wire (minimum $100), so payouts should land in a "fresh dollar" account. This report summarises official texts as they stood on 15 November 2026. It is research, not tax advice, and the open questions listed near the end need a written answer from the Ministry of Finance (MoF) or a Lebanese tax adviser.

## Ten terms to know before the numbers

The rules below use a handful of technical words. This table explains them in plain language for readers who are not tax specialists; the rest of the report uses them as defined here.

| Term | What it means | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax (ضريبة الدخل) | Tax on what you earn in a year after costs | You owe it in Lebanon on your app profit; no store pays it for you |
| VAT / TVA (الضريبة على القيمة المضافة) | A tax added to the price of a sale; the seller collects it from the buyer and passes it to the government | Lebanon's rate is 11%; the stores collect other countries' VAT, not Lebanon's |
| Merchant of record / commissionaire | The company that legally makes the sale at checkout, issues the receipt and deals with the sales tax. A commissionaire sells in its own name on someone else's behalf | Apple plays this role through its Irish company for most of the world, Lebanon included; Google does so only in Europe |
| Zero-rated export ("exempt with right of deduction") | A sale used abroad on which you charge 0% VAT, while staying inside the VAT system | You file VAT returns showing 0% and can reclaim VAT you paid on your costs |
| Withholding tax | Tax the payer deducts from your money before paying you, on behalf of a government | Brazil, Vietnam and Taiwan take a cut before Apple or Google pay you |
| W-8BEN | A US form on which you certify that you are not a US person | Without it, US rules let the store keep up to 30% |
| Tax treaty | An agreement between two countries that reduces double taxation | The US and Lebanon have none, so there is no reduced treaty rate to claim |
| Lump-sum vs real profit | Two ways of working out taxable profit: a fixed percentage of revenue set by the Ministry, or actual revenue minus actual costs taken from your books | Lump-sum is your default; real profit has to be requested before the end of January |
| Fresh dollars | Dollars that reached a Lebanese bank from abroad after 17 November 2019, kept separate from the frozen pre-crisis deposits | Payouts must land here so you can withdraw and transfer them |
| LBP 89,500 | The official Banque du Liban (BDL) rate per US dollar, used to convert amounts for tax | Every Lebanese tax band and fine in this report is converted at this rate |

## Apple and Google collect VAT almost everywhere except Lebanon

### Apple sells in its own name and taxes 94 regions, but Lebanon is not one of them

Under Apple's Developer Program License Agreement you are the **principal**: you own the app and you are the seller in law. Apple acts for you in one of two ways. In the US, Canada, Australia, Latin America and Japan it is your **agent**. For a long list of regions that **includes Lebanon**, the EU, the UK, the Gulf, Egypt, Türkiye and India, **Apple Distribution International Ltd (ADI, Cork, Ireland)** is your **commissionaire**. Apple defines this as "an agent who purports to act on their own behalf and concludes agreements in their own name but acts on behalf of other persons" ([Apple DPLA, Schedule 2 and Exhibit A](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Apple is "solely responsible for the collection of all prices payable by End-Users" and issues the customers' invoices. Who collects the sales tax is decided by **Exhibit B**, last updated 27 August 2026. Its list of **94 regions** covers all 27 EU states, the UK, the US, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Oman, Bahrain, Egypt, Türkiye, India, Switzerland, Norway, Australia, New Zealand and Canada. It also covers Japan, Korea and several Asian countries for developers who are not resident there, which includes you. **Lebanon is not on it.** For unlisted regions Exhibit B §2 says: "Apple shall not collect and remit the taxes … You shall be solely responsible for the collection and remittance of such taxes as may be required by local law" ([Apple DPLA, Exhibit B](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Other notable regions missing from the list are Israel, Jordan, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Pakistan, Hong Kong, Brazil and Argentina. This research did not check what those countries' own laws ask of a foreign individual seller.

For Lebanese customers the practical result is simple. Apple reports Lebanese sales in its "Rest of World" region, which is kept in **US dollars**, so Lebanese buyers almost certainly pay in dollars ([App Store Connect: financial report regions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/reporting/financial-report-regions-and-currencies)). Apple adds no Lebanese tax to the price. Its commission is taken on the full price, and any Lebanese tax you owe "shall not reduce the commission": it comes out of your share ([Apple DPLA, Schedule 2 §3.4 and §3.6](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Apple also does **not** reprice auto-renewable subscriptions when a country's tax changes, so any new or higher tax comes out of what you receive unless you change the price yourself ([Apple Developer News, 27 Aug 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=artln8br)). You are responsible for choosing the correct tax category for your app in App Store Connect, and the tax consequences of a wrong choice fall on you ([App Store Connect: set a tax category](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/set-a-tax-category)). Apple adds local VAT on its own commission only for developers based in Canada, Chile, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam. A Lebanon-based developer pays no such tax on Apple's commission ([Apple DPLA, Exhibit C](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)).

### Google is the legal seller only in Europe, but pays the tax in far more countries

Google's Developer Distribution Agreement, in force since **15 September 2025**, says in §3.4 that Google is the merchant of record, "acting as Your agent", **only** for users in a list of **54 territories**: the EEA, the UK and related territories. "You are the merchant of record for Products You sell or make available via Google Play to all other users", and that includes the US, Türkiye and Lebanon ([Google Play DDA](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html); [Play Console Help: Merchant of Record](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7645364?hl=en)). Being merchant of record and paying the tax are separate questions, though. Google's tax page lists many more countries where Google "is responsible for determining, charging, and remitting" VAT or GST for developers based elsewhere. These include **Saudi Arabia (15%), the UAE (5%), Oman, Bahrain, Egypt, India, Japan, Korea, Canada, Mexico, Singapore, Vietnam, Türkiye** and about 40 others, plus the US states that have a sales tax ("Even if you're not located in the United States, this treatment will still apply") ([Play Console Help: Tax rates and VAT](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en)). The later verification against Google's live pages **corrects** the earlier research note on two points: Türkiye is covered, and in Saudi Arabia Google remits for every developer located outside the country.

**Lebanon appears nowhere on Google's tax page.** It is also missing from the list of countries where Google supports tax-inclusive prices. Google says the developer is responsible in Colombia, Peru, Switzerland, Tanzania and Zimbabwe as well. In any country where tax is your responsibility, "Google by default does not set any transaction taxes" unless you enter a rate ([Play Console Help: Understanding your tax responsibilities](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16408159?hl=en)). If you do enter one, Google charges it at checkout, "pass[es] on the entire tax amount collected to the developer, and calculate[s] the service fee off the net price of the product" ([Play Console Help: Tax rates and VAT](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en)). Lebanon is not a tax-inclusive country, so a rate you set would most likely be added on top of the price shown to the buyer.

### What this means for a seller based in Lebanon

You do not need to register for VAT in the EU, the UK, the Gulf, Egypt, India, Türkiye or the US states for sales made through the stores' own payment systems. The stores collect that tax and pay you the net amount. Three limits apply. First, and most important, **both stores leave Lebanese VAT entirely to you**, so the question "do they handle it for customers in Lebanon?" has a clear answer: no. Second, a few markets are covered by neither store, or by only one, and their rules for foreign sellers were not researched. Third, if you move buyers to alternative billing or to a web checkout where the stores allow it, the tax on those sales becomes yours. Google remits in the EU, UK, Australia and the US states only "for purchases through Google Play's billing system", and under Apple's new EU terms you handle the tax on sales made with alternative payments ([Play Console Help: Tax rates and VAT](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en); [Apple DPLA, Attachment 14](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Finally, neither store deals with income tax. Apple says that if you incur income tax "You shall be solely responsible for that tax". Google's agreement makes you, "as the principal to the transaction with the user, … solely responsible for compliance worldwide with all applicable laws" ([Apple DPLA, Exhibit C §15.3](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/); [Google Play DDA §11.3](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html)).

## Lebanon taxes the profit because the work happens in Lebanon

### Foreign customers do not make the income foreign

Article 3 of the Income Tax Law (Legislative Decree 144 of 1959) taxes individuals and companies, resident or not, "on all profits they realise in Lebanon". Article 2 covers the profits of commercial and liberal professions and "of any income-producing work", and adds that "no income is exempt except by an explicit legal text" ([Income Tax Law, Arts. 2–4, Lebanese University legal database](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=273621&LawID=185603&language=ar)). PwC's reading is that a profit is realised in Lebanon "if it was generated through an effort or activity exerted in Lebanon" ([PwC, Lebanon corporate taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)). An app built, updated and supported from Beirut therefore produces **Lebanese-source business profit** (taxed under "Title I", the part of the law for business and professional profits), even when every subscriber is abroad and the money comes from Ireland. (The earlier note placed this rule in Article 1; the verification found it in **Article 3**.) Moving the payout account abroad does not change where the profit comes from. Lebanon has no "global" income tax that adds all your income together: each type of income is taxed under its own chapter, so a salary from a job would be taxed separately ([PwC, individual income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/income-determination)). The 2% tax on revenue from "services provided online" that the MoF proposed in 2023–24 was **never enacted**. The verification checked the tax articles of the 2024 budget (Law 324) and the 2026 budget (Law 40) and found no such tax ([Law 324/2024, Chapter 3](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=341580&LawID=330076&language=ar); [Law 40/2026, MoF PDF](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1504)).

No MoF ruling says whether selling app subscriptions counts as a "non-commercial" (liberal) profession or as a commercial activity. The tax scale for an individual is the same either way. What the label may change is whether you must also enter the Commercial Register, and which lump-sum percentage applies. That is one of the questions to ask in writing.

### Lump-sum by default, real profit by choice

Article 10 of the Income Tax Law tells anyone in a non-commercial profession to "declare his total revenues, unless he asks to be assessed on real profit". Article 12 lets "any person" ask for real-profit assessment, "provided the request is filed before the end of January of the tax year", and adds that "whoever chose real profit may not ask to go back" ([Income Tax Law, Arts. 6–30](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=273623&LawID=185603&language=ar)). On 24 December 2025, MoF Notice 4913 moved everyone on the old "estimated profit" method to lump-sum from 1 January 2026. It requires them to "register electronically, keep the books required of lump-sum taxpayers, declare the year's results before 1 February of the following year, issue invoices, and declare stamp duty on invoices (form G20) and pay it (form S17) within 15 days". A small-taxpayer route back to estimated profit closed on 31 March 2026 ([MoF Notice 4913](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1457)). Under lump-sum, your taxable profit is your revenue multiplied by a percentage that an MoF committee sets for each kind of activity (Arts. 21 and 23). The table in force is in MoF Decision 1375 of 2004, but the database does not reproduce the table itself ([Decision 1375/2004](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/Law.aspx?lawId=210581&language=ar)). **No verified percentage for software or app publishing exists in the sources.** The "50%" figure that circulates is unconfirmed. The texts also do not say whether "total revenues" means the price customers pay or the smaller amount Apple and Google pay you.

Real profit taxes actual revenue minus actual, documented costs: store commissions (if you record sales at the customer price), developer programme fees, hosting, software subscriptions, contractors and the depreciation of computers. It needs proper books. It is worth choosing if the MoF's lump-sum percentage turns out to be higher than your real margin. One wrinkle: Article 11 makes real profit **compulsory** for certain categories, and item 5 lists "exporters, importers, wholesalers". No source says whether a developer selling digital subscriptions abroad is an "exporter" in this sense, and PwC's summary mentions only importers of goods ([PwC, Lebanon corporate taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)). For tax year 2027, the real-profit request must be filed **before 31 January 2027**.

### Rates: 4% to 25%, after an allowance of about $5,000

Article 46 of Law 324/2024 sets the scale for "profits of commercial, industrial and non-commercial professions", and Article 49 sets the family allowances. Both apply "to real or lump-sum profit" ([Law 324/2024, Arts. 46 and 49](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=341580&LawID=330076&language=ar)). The verification confirmed that neither the 2025 budget (Decree 56/2025) nor Law 40/2026 changed these figures ([Decree 56/2025](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/Law.aspx?lawId=334856&language=ar); [PwC, individual deductions](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/deductions)). Each band applies to the part of taxable profit (profit minus allowances) that falls inside it.

| Taxable profit band (LBP) | ≈ USD at 89,500 | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 540 million | up to $6,034 | 4% |
| 540 million – 1.44 billion | $6,034 – $16,089 | 7% |
| 1.44 – 3.24 billion | $16,089 – $36,201 | 12% |
| 3.24 – 6.24 billion | $36,201 – $69,721 | 16% |
| 6.24 – 13.5 billion | $69,721 – $150,838 | 21% |
| Above 13.5 billion | above $150,838 | 25% |

The allowances are **LBP 450 million (≈ $5,028)** for the taxpayer, another **LBP 225 million (≈ $2,514)** for a married taxpayer whose spouse does not work (if both spouses work, each gets the single allowance), and **LBP 45 million (≈ $503)** per child, for up to five children. The examples below assume a single person whose real profit (or lump-sum profit) equals the figure shown.

| Annual profit | Tax (LBP) | Tax (≈ USD) | Share of profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10,000 | 17.8 million | $199 | 2.0% |
| $30,000 | 180.0 million | $2,011 | 6.7% |
| $60,000 | 569.4 million | $6,362 | 10.6% |
| $100,000 | 1,255.2 million | $14,025 | 14.0% |

A married taxpayer with a non-working spouse and two children would pay about **$1,589** on the same $30,000. The bands are fixed in pounds and are not adjusted for inflation, so their dollar value holds only while the official rate stays at 89,500.

### Pay in pounds or dollars, convert at the official rate

The verification **corrects** one point here. The earlier note read the 2024 budget as requiring tax on dollar income to be paid in dollars. The text of Law 324 Article 15, as replaced by Law 40 Article 22, says "taxes and fees are collected in LBP". It allows foreign-currency payment only for a closed list (bank interest, foreign shares and bonds, petroleum companies, some fees), and business profit tax is not on it. Since February 2026, though, Law 40 Article 22(ج) **allows** you to pay any tax due in pounds "in foreign currency (USD or EUR), converting at the effective rate set by BDL" ([Law 40/2026, pp. 33–34](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1504); [Law 324/2024, Art. 15](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=341580&LawID=330076&language=ar)). Amounts received in dollars are converted into pounds at BDL's effective rate. PwC (review of 1 July 2026) gives 89,500 as the current official rate ([PwC, corporate other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/other-taxes)). BDL's own website could not be reached, and no MoF instruction says which date's rate applies to a profit return (date of each sale, yearly average or year-end), so keep Apple's and Google's monthly statements, which show the exchange rate and payment date.

Lump-sum returns are due **before 1 February** (Art. 17) and real-profit returns **before 1 April** (Art. 13), and PwC notes that "the same deadlines for tax returns apply for tax payments" ([PwC, individual tax administration](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/tax-administration)). Lump-sum individuals file electronically on **form F1 (ف1)**, the personal income tax return, together with **form F3 (ف3)**, a statement of revenues and expenses. The MoF extended the 2025 lump-sum deadline to **16 March 2026** ([MoF Decision 286/1](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1526)). Both points correct the earlier note: the "R5/R7" forms it named are payroll forms, and its "27 February" date was the first extension, which a later decision superseded. PwC also lists a small annual lump-sum licence fee that every income taxpayer pays; its amounts (LBP 50,000–550,000 depending on the method) may be out of date ([PwC, individual other issues](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/other-issues)).

### A company rarely beats staying an individual

A Lebanese SAL or SARL company pays a flat **17%** on profit, and dividends paid out to you bear another **10%** ([PwC, Lebanon corporate taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)). On $100,000 of profit paid out in full, that is about **$25,300**, against about **$14,000** for an individual, and a company also costs more to run. An offshore company pays a flat **LBP 50 million (≈ $559)** a year instead of corporate tax. But its "business objectives … are limited" ([PwC, tax credits and incentives](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/tax-credits-and-incentives)), and the developer would still be doing the work inside Lebanon, which sits uneasily with the territorial rule above. Do not consider that route without a lawyer's written opinion.

## The exporter rule probably puts you in the VAT system from your first sale

### Two turnover thresholds, and one rule that overrides them

Article 3 of the VAT Law (Law 379/2001), as replaced by Article 14 of Law 324/2024, makes registration compulsory once turnover exceeds **LBP 5 billion (≈ $55,900)** "for a period of between one and four consecutive previous quarters". Voluntary registration is allowed above **LBP 1 billion (≈ $11,200)** over the same one-to-four-quarter window ([Law 324/2024, pp. 23–24, MoF PDF](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1135)). The verification corrected the earlier note, which said "4 consecutive quarters" for the voluntary threshold. Exports count towards turnover (Art. 4), and Law 40/2026 left Article 3 untouched. The MoF's own registration web page still shows an old LBP 150 million threshold and should not be relied on.

The rule that matters most is Article 3(4): "The exporter and the importer who … supplies goods **or provides services** that are taxable or exempt with right of deduction are subject to the tax, **whatever their turnover**" ([Law 324/2024, p. 24](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1135)). On 19 December 2017 the MoF read the same wording, then in force, as applying to "export or import operations **of goods or services**, whatever the turnover", with registration due "within two months of their first export or import" ([MoF notice of 19/12/2017](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/Law.aspx?lawId=274856)). **This reverses the earlier research note**, which guessed that "exporters" probably meant customs operators handling goods. On the law and the only official reading of it, a developer whose sales are exports of services must register for VAT from the first export, whatever the amount. No notice since 2024 repeats that reading, and none mentions app stores, so a written question to the VAT directorate is still worth sending. Staying unregistered carries real risk. In January 2026 the MoF reminded taxpayers that non-registration counts as tax evasion and said it would "pursue taxpayers who failed to register … recover the taxes and fines due and refer them to the Financial Public Prosecutor" ([MoF Notice 4/ص1, January 2026](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1460)).

### Foreign sales are taxed at 0%, if you can prove they were used abroad

Under the VAT Law a service is supplied in Lebanon "if the service is used within Lebanese territory" (Art. 14), and Article 19(1) exempts "supplies of services used outside Lebanese territory" ([VAT Law, Arts. 2–15](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=269924&LawID=203155&language=ar); [Arts. 19–21](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=269928&LawID=203155&language=ar)). The MoF describes these exempt-with-deduction operations as "operations taxed at 0%" ([MoF, Levying the VAT](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Levying-the-VAT.aspx)). The burden of proof is on you. Decree 7284/2002 limits the exemption to services "**proven** to be used outside Lebanese territory". Decree 7309/2002, Article 6, deems a service used in Lebanon "until the contrary is proven, as soon as one of the parties to the contract resides in Lebanon", and you reside in Lebanon ([Decree 7284/2002](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=199553&LawID=197314&language=ar); [Decree 7309/2002](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=199538&LawID=197299&language=ar)). Your proof will be the stores' sales reports broken down by country, the Apple and Google agreements, and the wires arriving from abroad. The verification corrects two points in the earlier note: the law has no separate "non-resident customer" condition (the test is where the service is used), and "Article 30" is the refund article, not the export article.

### The slice sold to subscribers in Lebanon is the unresolved 11%

No MoF ruling covers app-store income, so subscribers inside Lebanon can be read two ways. If your customer in law is Apple or Google, both non-resident companies, then everything you sell is an export and taxed at 0%. If your customers are the individual subscribers, those who live in Lebanon use the service in Lebanon, and a registered developer owes **11%** on those sales. My reading, which is an inference and not a ruling, is that the answer may differ by store. For Apple, ADI sells to Lebanese buyers "in their own name" as commissionaire. The VAT Law's Article 12 treats an agent acting in its own name as the one making the supply, although that article was written for agents registered in Lebanon. That gives a respectable argument that Apple's Lebanese sales are exports to ADI. For Google, the agreement says plainly that outside the 54 European territories "You are the merchant of record", which puts your Google Play sales to Lebanese users closer to ordinary domestic sales ([Google Play DDA §3.4](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html)). The Lebanese share of a typical app is small, and both stores report sales by country, so the amount at stake can be measured. If you are registered and treat the Google slice as domestic, you can set a custom 11% rate for Lebanon in Play Console; the buyer then pays it at checkout and Google passes it to you. Apple offers no way to add Lebanese VAT, so it would come out of your share. Never charge VAT before you are registered: a non-registered person who issues an invoice showing VAT is fined **three times the VAT shown** ([MoF, VAT fines table](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Companies/FinesOff/Pages/VAT.aspx)).

The standard rate is still **11%** (Art. 25). The Cabinet approved 12% in February 2026, but the Finance Minister said this needs a law, and no such law or MoF notice had appeared by 15 November 2026 ([VAT Law, Art. 25](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=269931&LawID=203155&language=ar); [The National, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/02/17/anger-in-lebanon-as-government-raises-vat-and-petrol-prices/); [vatupdate, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/02/26/lebanon-plans-vat-hike-from-11-to-12-pending-parliamentary-approval/)). In September 2026 the Finance Minister said the ministry is working on taxing foreign digital services used in Lebanon, including **paid apps** ([al-Marsad, Sept 2026](https://almarsadonline.com/260178)). No text has been published, and the 2027 draft budget shows no VAT article on the subject, although that reading of a partly garbled PDF is not conclusive ([MoF, 2027 draft budget](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1644)). If such a tax were enacted and made the platforms collect it, the Lebanese-subscriber question would probably move from you to them.

### Services you buy from abroad, quarterly returns and refunds

Article 40(2) makes "every person resident in Lebanon who uses there a service acquired from a party resident abroad" declare and pay the VAT on it "whatever its value". This "reverse charge" (you pay the VAT the foreign seller did not charge) is made in the periodic return if you are registered, or by a special return if you are not (Decree 7309, Art. 10, which settles a gap left open in the earlier note) ([VAT Law, Arts. 33–41](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=269934&LawID=203155&language=ar); [Decree 7309/2002](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=199538&LawID=197299&language=ar)). For a registered developer this covers the $99 Apple fee, the Google fee, cloud hosting and software subscriptions. You declare 11% on each and normally deduct the same amount in the same return, so it costs nothing. The exception is VAT on "water, electricity, telecommunications, **internet** and petrol", which Law 40/2026 made non-deductible from 2026; input VAT on cars is capped at a car value of $30,000 ([Law 40/2026, Art. 30](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1504)). Returns cover calendar quarters, and since Law 40/2026 Article 51 the return **and payment are due within one month** of the quarter's end, instead of 20 days ([Law 40/2026, p. 79](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1504)). The periodic return is form ق1-2, and a special return, ق6-2, covers a period of late registration ([MoF guide, ق1-2](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Taxation/Na/VAT/Tifsir/%D9%821-2%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD%20%D8%AF%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A.pdf); [MoF guide, ق6-2](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Taxation/Na/VAT/Tifsir/%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84%20%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD%20%28%D9%826-2%29.pdf)). Exporters can claim back excess VAT after any quarter, but only if the claim is **at least LBP 200 million (≈ $2,235)**. That figure is a minimum, not the "cap" PwC describes ([Law 40/2026, Art. 31](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1504)). A small exporter will therefore carry its credits forward rather than receive refunds. A VAT invoice must show your name, address and MoF number, the customer, a sequential number and date, the amount and the VAT rate ([VAT Law, Art. 38](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=269934&LawID=203155&language=ar)). Apple's agreement allows you to invoice ADI for the amounts due to you ([Apple DPLA, Exhibit C §16](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)), which gives you a clean export invoice.

## Registration, deadlines and penalties: 2026 raised the cost of staying unregistered

### One tax number, applied for within two months

Under Article 32 of the Tax Procedures Law (Law 44/2008), "anyone starting a taxable activity must inform the tax administration by a registration request filed **within two months** of starting". Under Article 34 the administration gives you "a single tax number for all taxes" ([Tax Procedures Law, Arts. 32–35](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=273817&LawID=246318&language=ar)). This **corrects** the "before 1 February" deadline in the MoF's 2003 guide that the earlier note relied on. Since Notice 4913, lump-sum taxpayers register electronically. Any later change of address, place of work or main activity must be reported within two months (Law 40/2026, Art. 18). Article 20 of the Income Tax Law requires a daily journal of receipts, numbered and initialled by a notary ([Income Tax Law, Arts. 6–30](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=273623&LawID=185603&language=ar)). PwC says books must be kept for ten years and that the administration has four years to collect what it is owed ([PwC, individual tax administration](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/tax-administration)). The MoF's Notice 7/ص1 of 2 January 2026 nonetheless tells unregistered lump-sum taxpayers to file start-of-activity declarations and returns "for 2018 through 2025", and recalls that non-registration is legally tax evasion ([MoF Notice 7/ص1](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1464)). An adviser should tell you how far back you actually need to go. Staying unregistered is getting harder anyway. Since October 2026 Apple's agreement says it "will report legally required information such as tax, sales, and contact details to tax authorities" ([Apple Developer News, 13 Oct 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=17mwd36z)). Lebanon also has legislation on the OECD's Common Reporting Standard (CRS), the system under which banks report foreign accounts to the account holder's home tax authority ([OECD, Lebanon CRS legislation](https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/crs-by-jurisdiction/legislation/Lebanon-PrimaryLegislation.pdf)). The registration form codes and the step-by-step use of the MoF e-services portal could not be confirmed.

| Obligation | Deadline | Form or note |
|---|---|---|
| Tax registration (one number for income tax and VAT) | Within 2 months of starting | Electronic; TPL Art. 32 |
| VAT registration as an exporter | Within 2 months of the first export | MoF 2017 notice; Art. 3(4) |

| Reduced-fine settlement window | Until 30 December 2026 | MoF Decision 642/1 |

The MoF extends deadlines often. On 11 November 2026 it extended the Q3-2026 VAT return and payment and issued rules applying Law 46 of 27 July 2026, which suspends legal deadlines. The verification could not open those PDFs, so the new end dates are unknown ([MoF, Q3-2026 VAT extension](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1657)).

### Penalties were raised in 2024 and multiplied in 2026

The earlier notes gave a late-filing fine of 5% a month and late-payment interest of 1% a month. The verification against the 2024 budget law **corrects both**. The rates below come from Law 324/2024 Articles 74, 75 and 77 and Law 40/2026 Articles 16 and 17, the last two applying "from fiscal year 2026" ([Law 324/2024, pp. 86–89](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1135); [Law 40/2026, pp. 25–27](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1504); [Tax Procedures Law, Arts. 107–117](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=273833&LawID=246318&language=ar)).

| Failure | Penalty for an individual | ≈ USD |
|---|---|---|
| Late return (income tax or VAT) | 10% of the tax due per month or part of a month, capped at 100%; minimum LBP 2.5 million from 2026 (LBP 750,000 before) | minimum ≈ $28 |
| Under-declared tax | 20% of the shortfall, same minimum | — |
| Late payment | 2% a month on self-assessed income tax; 3% a month on VAT and withheld taxes; part of a month counts as a full month | — |
| Not registering in time | Base fine LBP 300,000 (TPL Art. 107) × 25 from 2026 = LBP 7.5 million (inferred; the base amount was not cross-checked against the Official Gazette) | ≈ $84 |
| Charging VAT while not registered | 3 × the VAT shown on the invoice | — |

The fixed amounts are small in dollars. The real cost of staying unregistered is having VAT and income tax assessed for every past year, plus 10% a month up to 100% and late-payment interest on top, and possible referral to the Financial Public Prosecutor. It is not settled whether the LBP 2.5 million minimum applies to a late VAT return that shows no tax due, which is the normal case for a pure exporter. The 2026 budget also added a 1.5% advance payment at customs on imports by people who filed no income tax or VAT returns in the previous three years, which matters if you import equipment ([Orbitax, Lebanon Budget 2026 measures](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851)).

### Catching up before 30 December 2026 costs much less

MoF Decision 642/1 of 10 July 2026 cuts fines on tax assessment documents issued since 16 November 2022, "whether self-assessed or issued by the administration". Proportional fines are cut by **85%**, fixed fines by **60%** and late-payment fines by **75%**, on condition that the tax is paid together with the reduced fine **by 30 December 2026** ([MoF Decision 642/1](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1606)). The decision excludes VAT and payroll tax, which have their own schemes. The earlier VAT note said it covered VAT; the verification corrects that. The VAT scheme, Decision 834/1, was extended on 11 November 2026, but its new end date could not be read ([MoF, extension of Decision 834/1](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Pages/Pdf-Preview.aspx?dt=OL1658)). If you earned app income in 2024 or 2025 without registering, filing those returns now and paying before 30 December 2026 appears to qualify for the reduced fines. An adviser should confirm that late returns you file yourself count as "self-assessed" documents under the decision.

### Social security: nothing compulsory, and little on offer

The NSSF is built around employers and employees, so a freelancer working alone owes no compulsory contributions. A voluntary sickness and maternity scheme for the self-employed was created in 2002, but according to the ILO the NSSF has suspended new enrolment ([ILO, Extending social health protection in Lebanon](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@arabstates/@ro-beirut/documents/publication/wcms_780987.pdf)). The new pension system includes self-employed Lebanese residents who apply voluntarily ([ILO, Q&A on the new pension scheme](https://www.ilo.org/media/480606/download)), but no source confirms that it is enrolling them as of November 2026. Members of regulated professions must join their professional syndicate ([NRC, Lebanon self-employment guide](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/guidelines/guide-to-self-employment-and-small-business/lebanon-self-employment-and-small-business-guide_en.pdf)), but software development is not one of those professions. The verification pass did not re-check this section against official NSSF texts. In practice, health cover means private insurance. Employer NSSF registration becomes necessary only if you hire staff or pay yourself a salary through a company.

## No US treaty, but a correct W-8BEN keeps US withholding at zero

### The US form and why the missing treaty matters little

The IRS list of tax treaty countries jumps from "Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg" with no Lebanon ([IRS, US income tax treaties A to Z, reviewed 3 Jan 2026](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z)). The verification confirmed what the earlier notes had from secondary sources only. On the **W-8BEN** you state that you are an individual resident in Lebanon and **leave Part II (the treaty claim) blank**. Both stores require the form. Without a valid one, Google "may apply backup withholding at 24% or chapter 3 withholding at 30%". The form must be renewed "every 3 years" or whenever your circumstances change, and non-US payees receive a Form 1042-S in March ([Google Payments Center: US tax information](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/10349995?hl=en); [Play Console Help: merchant tax information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7163598?hl=en)). Apple may hold your money "in trust" until the tax documents are provided, and once a form is submitted it cannot be changed in App Store Connect ([Apple DPLA, Schedule 2 §3.3](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/); [App Store Connect: provide tax information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/provide-tax-information)). In 2026 some developers reported on Apple's forum that App Store Connect offered them the wrong form ([Apple Developer Forums thread 843940](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843940)). If that happens, contact Apple rather than sign the wrong form.

The missing treaty costs little because neither store treats ordinary app sales as US income of yours. Apple says: "You're the seller of copyrighted works, and we make payments to you for products or goods" ([App Store Connect: invoices and tax documents](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/manage-invoices-and-other-tax-documents)). An Apple Finance reply relayed on Apple's forum in 2020 says "application sales by non-U.S. developers … are not subject to U.S. tax withholding or reporting", so neither a treaty claim nor a US tax number is needed. It is an informal, older statement ([Apple Developer Forums thread 660025](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/660025)). Apple keeps the right to withhold where it "reasonably believes" tax is due (Schedule 2 §3.7). Google says a documented non-US person is taxed only "on income that is earned from US sources, for example if a non-US person performs services in the US". You certify under the DDA that your services are not performed in the US. Google also notes that subscription revenue "may be considered service income" ([Google Payments Center](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/10349995?hl=en); [Google Play DDA §3.6](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html); [Play Console Help: withholding tax](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608?hl=en)). Work done in Lebanon is therefore not US-source, and no US withholding should apply. That conclusion is an inference, because no Google page says it in so many words. Google Play Pass income is classed as royalties and could be withheld at 30% with no treaty relief. After your first payouts, check both stores' statements for any US withholding line.

### Brazil, Vietnam and Taiwan are where foreign tax really bites

Google deducts Brazilian income tax (IRRF) and a Brazilian levy called CIDE on all Brazilian sales when you are paid in a currency other than the real. Brazil designates **Lebanon** as a tax haven, so Google must "withhold an additional 10% IRRF". Treaty relief applies only to residents of countries with a Brazilian tax treaty, and Lebanon is not one ([Play Console Help: withholding tax](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608?hl=en)). The page no longer states the base rates, so the earlier note's "25%" figure is not confirmed. The same page lists 5% on gross sales to users in Vietnam for developers outside Vietnam, 3% on sales to users in Taiwan by default, and withholding on carrier-billed sales (charged to the buyer's phone bill) in Egypt (up to 20%), Kuwait (up to 5%), Myanmar and Sri Lanka. The verification **corrects** the earlier list in three ways: Saudi Arabia is no longer on the page; Kenya, Tanzania and India apply only to developers located there; and Vietnam is new. "The only way to opt-out of withholding taxes is to remove your app from distribution in the countries where withholding taxes apply". Google issues an annual statement of what it withheld. On Apple, Brazilian sales lose withholding on money sent out of Brazil, a **10% CIDE** since May 2025 and a **3.5% IOF** (a Brazilian financial transactions tax) since August 2025. Individual developers outside Vietnam pay a **5%** Vietnamese income tax, and Apple collects digital services taxes such as Türkiye's 5% and Tanzania's 3% ([Apple Developer News, 16 May 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=wim4cztw); [21 Aug 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=yo2104n5); [27 Aug 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=artln8br)).

### Lebanon gives no credit for any of it

PwC finds "no specific regulations concerning foreign tax credit in Lebanon", and says taxes paid to foreign governments on income earned in Lebanon are non-deductible ([PwC, tax credits and incentives](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/tax-credits-and-incentives)). Lebanon has signed 34 tax treaties with 32 countries ([IDAL, international agreements](https://investinlebanon.gov.lb/en/doing_business/legal_framework/international_agreements)), but none with the US or the UK ([HMRC manual DT11850](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt11850)), and no source shows one with Ireland or Singapore, where the stores' paying companies sit. Every dollar withheld abroad is therefore lost for good, and you still pay Lebanese tax on it. Brazil should be judged on what it actually pays you after all deductions: raise prices there, or leave the market if it does not pay.

## Both stores keep about 15% of a small developer's subscriptions

Apple's standard commission is **30%**, falling to **15%** on renewals once a subscriber has more than one year of paid service. The **Small Business Program** cuts it to **15% from the first day** for developers with no more than $1 million of proceeds in the previous calendar year, and for developers new to the store. You enrol as Account Holder, accept the Paid Apps Agreement and list any associated accounts. The rate applies from 15 days after the end of the Apple fiscal month in which you are approved. If your proceeds go over $1 million during a year, the standard rate applies for the rest of that year ([Apple, Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/); [Apple DPLA, Schedule 2 §3.4](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). One wording risk: the agreement grants the 15% rate on sales "to End-Users located in those regions listed in Exhibit B", which does not include Lebanon, while the programme page sets no regional limit. Check the commission on your Lebanese sales in your first financial report. Since 1 October 2026 the EU has its own terms: Apple's in-app purchase commission there is 26%, or 15% for Small Business Program members and for subscriptions after their first year. Japan and Brazil charge 21% plus a 5% payment fee, or 10% plus 5% under the programme ([Apple, updates for apps in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/); [Apple DPLA, Attachments 12 and 14](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). On the US storefront, apps may now link to outside payment, and the commission Apple may charge on those purchases is still being decided in court ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple takes its commission on the price after removing the taxes it collects ([Apple DPLA, Schedule 2 §3.4(a)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). A $4.99 subscription bought in Lebanon therefore earns you about **$4.24**, and a €9.99 subscription in an EU country with 20% VAT about **€7.08**. Membership costs **$99 a year**. In regions without an Apple Store Online, and Lebanon appears to be one, Apple asks for a credit card that can be charged in US dollars ([Apple, enrollment support](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/)).

Google charges the legacy rate in Lebanon, the rest of the Middle East and most other countries: "15% for automatically renewing subscription products … regardless of revenue earned". This lasts until the new model reaches the "rest of world" **by 30 September 2027**. Buyers in the EEA, the UK and the US (since 30 June 2026), and in Australia and **Japan** (since 30 September 2026), already pay under the new model: a **10% service fee plus a 5% billing fee** when Google Play Billing is used. South Korea, **Türkiye** and **South Africa** switch on 31 December 2026, and billing fees for markets after the first group are "forthcoming" ([Play Console Help: service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en); [Play Console Help: lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)). This **corrects** the earlier note, which put Japan with Korea and left out Türkiye and South Africa. Where alternative billing or web links are allowed, only the 10% service fee applies, but you then run the payment yourself and owe the sales tax on it ([Android Developers Blog, 24 Jun 2026](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/expanded-billing-choice-and-lower-fees-on-google-play)). New personal Play accounts must run a closed test with "a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days" before they can publish ([Play Console Help: testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)). The one-time registration fee is $25, a figure confirmed only by a secondary source ([ConsoleMint](https://consolemint.com/google-play-console-price/)).

## Payouts arrive as US-dollar wires that must land in fresh money

### Google: a Lebanese bank account, in dollars, from $100

Google's table marks Lebanon as supported for both developer and merchant registration. It shows the default currency as "LBP[1]", and the footnote says these developers "will be paid in USD or EUR". Its bank page adds that "Merchants in Lebanon receive payments via wire transfer from the United States. Payouts will arrive in US Dollars" ([Play Console Help: supported locations](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9306917?hl=en); [Play Console Help: merchant bank account](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161440?hl=en)). The bank account must be "in the same country or region where the merchant account is registered", and "You can't change your business location country" later ([Play Console Help: create a payments profile](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161426?hl=en)). In practice that means a **Lebanese bank account in dollars**. The **$100 minimum** is a firm rule; the earlier note called it a recommendation. Payouts go out "around the 15th of the following month", and your bank's wire fees "may range from US$0 to US$50 or more" ([Play Console Help: wire transfer payouts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2700656?hl=en); [Play Console Help: order processing and payouts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/137997?hl=en)).

### Apple: more freedom over where the bank is

Apple pays by wire "within 45 days of the last day of the fiscal month", in one payment per currency. Countries not in its table, Lebanon among them, have a **$40** minimum, while euro accounts at EU banks and dirham accounts in the UAE have almost none ([App Store Connect: receiving payments](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/overview-of-receiving-payments); [App Store Connect: minimum payment threshold](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/reporting/minimum-payment-threshold)). Apple asks for the bank's country separately from your own, and says "the account holder name … doesn't have to match the legal entity name for your agreement". It also warns that if you can't find your bank in the list, "Apple can't send payments to that bank" ([App Store Connect: banking information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/reporting/banking-information)). No Apple page confirms that Lebanese banks appear in that list, so check in App Store Connect. Developers on Apple's forum report payments stuck in "Processing" when the account holder differed from the person on the agreement, so use an account in your own name.

### The Lebanese banking backdrop

Lebanon has **no capital-controls law**. Banks have applied informal limits since October 2019, but money deposited after the crisis is treated separately and "may generally be transferred abroad" ([US State Department, 2026 Investment Climate Statement](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/lebanon)). Under BDL Basic Circular 165 of April 2023, money transferred from abroad after 17 November 2019 counts as **fresh money** and clears through dedicated BDL accounts ([RegAlert, BDL decision 13548](https://regalert.today/document/5e300c38-625a-4e58-849f-9d32b45deb57/text)). Pre-crisis deposits, by contrast, are still limited to small yearly amounts ([RegAlert, BDL decision 13771](https://regalert.today/document/c4efb249-e7e6-46f4-a25b-d9fbfaa1681c/text)). So the account that receives your store payouts must be a **fresh USD account**, never an old one. Lebanon has been on the FATF "grey list" of countries under increased monitoring since October 2024. It was kept there in June 2026 ([FATF list of 19 June 2026](https://fiu.rks-gov.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FATF-Jurisdictions-under-Increased-Monitoring-19-June-2026.pdf)) and, according to consistent secondary reports, again at the October 2026 plenary ([ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-october-2026-four-grey-list-exits-a-virtual-asset-review-and-a-new-focus-on-scam-compounds/)). The EU added Lebanon to its list of high-risk countries in 2025 ([EUR-Lex, Delegated Regulation 2025/1184](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/1184/oj/eng)). Expect banks to ask where the money comes from, and expect some delays on wires from Apple's and Google's companies. Keep the stores' statements ready as proof. The bank resolution law, amended in August 2026, is on hold until the "Gap Law" on deposit losses is passed, and the IMF is reviewing it ([Beirut Core, Aug 2026](https://www.beirutcore.com/briefs/source/SF-LBN-LEGAL-2026-08); [The National, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/10/01/imf-lebanon-bank-resolution-law/)). Since the sources do not settle how a future bank rescue would treat fresh deposits, do not let large balances build up; move them on regularly. US sanctions on Lebanon target specific people and networks; the country itself is not under a blanket embargo ([Model Diplomat, OFAC Lebanon sanctions](https://modeldiplomat.com/learn/glossary/ofac-lebanon-sanctions)). Treasury did target unlicensed money exchangers in November 2025, so convert money only through licensed banks ([US Treasury via GlobalSecurity, 6 Nov 2025](https://www.globalsecurity.org/security/library/news/2025/11/sec-251106-treasury01.htm)). The Wise card is not available to Lebanese residents ([The Currency Shop](https://www.thecurrencyshop.com.au/guides/using-the-wise-card-in-lebanon)), and reports on whether Payoneer works in Lebanon conflict ([Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/people-ask/does-payoneer-work-in-lebanon)). Holding a foreign bank account is legal, but it does not move the income out of Lebanese tax, and any interest it earns must be declared separately as foreign investment income.

## Conclusion

The common belief that "the stores handle the tax" is right about foreign VAT and wrong about everything Lebanese. At indie scale that does not mean a heavy tax bill. Income tax stays under 10% of profit up to about $50,000, and VAT on foreign sales is zero. The real exposure is the cost of not complying: back-assessment of every past year, fines that 2026 multiplied by 25, and a ministry that now formally treats non-registration as evasion. The money at stake in VAT is small (the Lebanese-subscriber slice), but the obligation to register appears to start at the first dollar. Registering early is therefore the cheap option, and the 30 December 2026 settlement window makes this the least costly moment to deal with past years.

The two stores are not interchangeable, either. Apple sells to Lebanese buyers in its own name through Ireland and lets you choose the country of your bank. Google leaves you as the legal seller to Lebanese buyers, ties your bank account to Lebanon and pays in dollars by wire. So Apple's Lebanese sales have the better argument for being treated as exports, while Google's are the more likely to carry 11%, and Google's payouts depend more on the Lebanese banking system. Three dates will shape the next year: 31 January 2027 for choosing the tax method, 1 February 2027 for the first lump-sum return, and Google's switch to its new fees for the rest of the world by 30 September 2027. In the background, two pending measures could change the picture: a 12% VAT law and a Lebanese tax on foreign digital services. The second would probably make Apple and Google collect Lebanese VAT themselves, which would take the Lebanese-subscriber question off your desk.
