# Alternatives to a Lebanese bank for store payouts, and to store-only billing, for an individual developer resident in Lebanon (as of 15 November 2026)

> **Method note for the report writer (important).** Direct page fetching was blocked in this session for every provider host tried (stripe.com, docs.lemonsqueezy.com, www.paddle.com, www.revenuecat.com all failed with "host outside the session's allowed network domains"). This is the same block the earlier tax research hit. **Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result snippets and summaries of the cited pages, not from reading them in full.** Treat all of it as *unverified until checked on the live page*, especially country lists, which the snippets often cut off. Labels: **[official]** = the provider's own page or docs; **[secondary]** = blogs, aggregators, competitors, forums, news. "Accessed" means 15 Nov 2026 for every source. A publication date is given where the result showed one. Competitor blogs (Dodo Payments, Fungies, Freemius, Skydo, Payhip) are marked as such because they have an interest in the comparison.
>
> **Already established by the earlier work (not repeated in detail):** Google pays Lebanon-profile merchants by USD wire from the US and requires a bank in the profile country. Apple asks for the bank's country separately and says the account holder "doesn't have to match the legal entity name". Store payouts should land in a **fresh USD** account. Lebanon is FATF grey-listed (June and, per secondary reports, October 2026) and on the EU high-risk list. The Wise card is not available to Lebanese residents, and Payoneer's status is disputed. Lebanon taxes profit from work done in Lebanon whatever the customers' or the bank's country. See `/home/user/APP/reports/Lebanon app subscription taxes.md` ("Payouts arrive as US-dollar wires…", "The Lebanese banking backdrop", "Lebanon taxes the profit because the work happens in Lebanon") and `/home/user/APP/research_notes/Lebanon app subscription taxes/treaties_withholding_payouts.md` §4–5.

## A. Where Apple/Google payouts can go other than a Lebanese bank (foreign bank in own name; Wise, Payoneer, Revolut, others)

### Takeaway
**Google:** no alternative for an individual in Lebanon. The payout bank must be in the payments-profile country, which is Lebanon and cannot be changed. **Apple:** in principle it accepts a bank in another country, but that only helps if the developer can actually open one. None of the fintechs checked can be shown, from an official page, to open accounts for Lebanese residents: Revolut appears closed to them, Wise is unconfirmed, Payoneer is disputed. A real foreign bank account in the developer's own name, for example in the UAE or Cyprus, is the only realistic non-Lebanese destination, and only for Apple.

### Cited Findings
**Google Play: the bank must match the payments-profile country (confirms the earlier note)**
- Google's help says the bank account must be in the same country as the business location on the payments profile. Its test-deposit troubleshooting lists "Your bank isn't located in the same country you entered in your payments profile" as a cause of failure. — [Play Console Help: Create a payments profile (7161426)](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161426?hl=en); [Payments Center Help 7161426](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7161426); [Play Console Help 7161649](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161649?hl=en) **[official, via search summary; accessed 15 Nov 2026]**

- After a merchant payments profile is set up, its country cannot be changed. If the legal business location changes, you must create a new payments profile and transfer the apps to it. PO boxes are not allowed. — [Play Console Help 7162811](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7162811?hl=en); [Payments Center Help 9037880](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/9037880) **[official, via search summary]**

**A bank abroad in the developer's own name (UAE example)**
- In the UAE no Central Bank rule requires a resident, but banks decide case by case and in practice often expect a resident signatory. Free-zone authorities issue licences but do not approve bank accounts. — [private.law wiki: UAE company bank account](https://wiki.private.law/en/uae-company-bank-account); [Flying Colour: non-resident bank accounts in the UAE](https://flyingcolour.net/blog/all-you-need-to-know-about-non-resident-bank-account-openings-in-the-uae) **[secondary, service providers]**
- No source specific to Lebanese nationals opening personal non-resident accounts in the UAE, Cyprus, France or Turkey was found in this session. The earlier note expects enhanced due diligence at EU banks because of the EU high-risk listing.

### Inferences
- **Google route is fixed.** An individual whose business location is Lebanon must be paid into a Lebanese bank, a fresh USD account. Pointing Google at a Wise/UAE/Cyprus account would mean declaring a different business location, which Google says must be the real legal location. Only a company genuinely located abroad could do that (see D).
- **Apple route is open in principle.** Apple ties processing to the bank branch's country, not the developer's (earlier report). A UAE dirham account, or a euro account at an EU bank, would also get Apple's near-zero minimum payment. The binding constraint is not Apple but whether a bank abroad will onboard a Lebanon resident.
- **Fintechs do not currently solve this for a Lebanon resident.** Revolut appears closed to residents. Wise is not shown to be open (old anecdotes only; card unavailable). Payoneer is disputed and has reports of rejected withdrawals. Even where an account works, receiving Apple wires into fintech USD details depends on Apple's bank list recognising the fintech's partner bank. That was not verified.
- **If a fintech account is opened, use only real details.** A Lebanese-resident account opened on a foreign address would be misrepresentation and would risk closure with the funds held. The Nomadgate anecdote shows closures do happen.

### Gaps
- Wise's full "countries where Wise can't be used" list and its account-eligibility page could not be opened, so Lebanon's status is unconfirmed either way.
- No official Payoneer country page for Lebanon was found. It is unknown whether Payoneer issues USD receiving accounts to Lebanese residents in 2026, or whether "withdraw to local bank" is offered for Lebanon.
- Not verified: whether Apple's bank list in App Store Connect accepts Wise/Payoneer receiving-account details (routing via their US partner banks) for a developer whose agreement is in Lebanon.
- No source found on Cyprus, France or Turkey banks' 2026 policies on Lebanese-resident individuals.

## B. Selling subscriptions on the web: payment processors and merchants of record (MoR) for a seller resident in Lebanon

### Takeaway
**Stripe and PayPal are not usable** by a Lebanon-resident seller. For PayPal the evidence is secondary only. Several Stripe-based tools that depend on them (Polar, Creem, RevenueCat Billing) are therefore unavailable too. The two merchants of record whose official pages do not exclude Lebanon are **Lemon Squeezy**, which lists Lebanon for bank payouts, and **Paddle**, whose seller exclusion list does not appear to include Lebanon. The snippets show both only in part. Both charge about **5% + 50¢** and collect sales tax and VAT worldwide as the legal seller. Local gateways (Areeba, Whish Pay) mainly reach customers in Lebanon and are reported to settle in LBP or into a wallet.

**Stripe (direct account)**
- No official Stripe page could be opened. Search results contain no Stripe statement that Lebanon is supported. Third-party lists and forums say it is not. It says these lists do not decide Atlas incorporation eligibility. — [Stripe Support: Treasury account availability for Stripe Atlas businesses](https://support.stripe.com/questions/treasury-account-availability-for-stripe-atlas-businesses) **[official, via search summary]**

### Inferences
- **The realistic web-billing route for an individual in Lebanon is an MoR that pays out by bank wire to Lebanon: Lemon Squeezy, or Paddle if it accepts the seller.** Both act as the legal seller to the customer and handle VAT/GST/sales tax in the countries they cover. The developer receives a payout, as with the stores, at about 5% + 50¢ instead of 15%. On a $5/month subscription that is about $0.75 (15%) against the 15% store commission. The small-ticket penalty of the fixed 50¢ matters for cheap monthly plans.
- **Stripe direct, Stripe Managed Payments, RevenueCat Billing, Polar and Creem all need a Stripe-supported seller country.** None is open to an individual in Lebanon, unless the developer uses a company abroad (D).
- **Lemon Squeezy carries a continuity risk.** It is owned by Stripe, whose newer MoR product is built for Stripe accounts. If Lemon Squeezy were wound down, a Lebanon-based seller might have no Stripe-side migration path. Nothing found announces a shutdown; this is a risk, not a fact.
- **Paddle's Lebanon "USD | Exclusive" entry is about buyers.** It means Paddle sells to customers in Lebanon with tax added on top. It says nothing about sellers, and it does not show whether Paddle remits Lebanese VAT (the earlier report found Apple does not).
- **Lebanese VAT angle (to check with an adviser):** with an MoR, the developer's supply is arguably to the foreign MoR, not to consumers, much like the store model. That could support export (0%) treatment under the earlier report's exporter rule.
- **Local gateways do not replace an MoR for a global subscription business.** Areeba reportedly settles in LBP, not fresh USD. Whish Pay only reaches Whish wallet holders, mostly in Lebanon. They may suit Lebanese customers paying locally, but they leave all foreign tax compliance with the developer.

### Gaps
- Paddle's full seller exclusion list and Lemon Squeezy's full bank-payout list could not be opened. Both need a check on the live page or with sales/support before committing. Neither provider's KYC requirements (documents, sole-trader acceptance, proof of address) for Lebanese individuals were found.
- Paddle payout methods and minimums for Lebanon (wire vs PayPal) were not found.
- No official Stripe or PayPal page was retrieved. The "not supported" conclusions rest on secondary and community sources.
- OMT Pay merchant services, Areeba's onboarding of sole traders, and whether any local gateway settles in fresh USD were not confirmed.
- 2Checkout/Verifone: whether Lebanon is on the merchant restricted list is unknown.

## C. RevenueCat and other tools that combine store and web billing

### Takeaway
RevenueCat **does not take or pay out money**. It records subscriptions bought through Apple, Google, Stripe or Paddle and unlocks them across platforms. The developer is still paid by each store and by the web processor. For a Lebanon resident, only its **Paddle** integration is plausible, since its own "RevenueCat Billing" runs on Stripe. Web Purchase Links through Paddle are reported as "invite only". Web is included in RevenueCat's normal price: free to about $2,500 of monthly tracked revenue, then about 1% (secondary).

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat Web supports three billing engines: **RevenueCat Billing** (its own engine, with **Stripe** as the payment gateway), **Stripe Billing**, and **Paddle Billing**, each using the developer's own account. — [RevenueCat Docs: Web overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/overview); [RevenueCat Docs: Web Billing overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/web-billing/overview) **[official, via search summary]**
- With Paddle Billing, Paddle is the merchant of record and handles tax and compliance. **Web Purchase Links** are supported for RevenueCat Billing and Stripe Billing. For Paddle the docs' table says "Supported (invite only)". — [RevenueCat Docs: Web Billing overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/web-billing/overview) **[official, via search summary]**
- "RevenueCat Web is included in RevenueCat's price; there are no additional RevenueCat fees to support subscriptions and purchases on the web." Processor fees are separate, e.g. "Stripe transaction fee: 2.9% + 30¢ (US pricing)" plus optional Stripe Tax. — [RevenueCat Docs: Web overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/overview) **[official, via search summary]**
- **Conflict:** a Japanese RevenueCat Billing page and a comparison site say RevenueCat Billing purchases also count toward the standard plan fee, e.g. 1% above $2,500 monthly revenue. — [RevenueCat (JP): Billing](https://www.revenuecat.com/jp/billing.md); [Costbench: Paddle vs RevenueCat pricing (2026)](https://www.costbench.com/compare/paddle-vs-revenuecat/) **[official JP page + secondary]**
- Trials are not yet supported in RevenueCat purchase flows for Stripe Billing. — [RevenueCat Docs: Web Billing overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/web-billing/overview) **[official, via search summary]**
- RevenueCat's own blog argues the case against pushing users from the app to the web ("why you shouldn't use app to web"). — [RevenueCat blog](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/why-you-shouldnt-use-app-to-web/) **[official blog; content not read]**

### Inferences
- RevenueCat changes **who keeps track of subscriptions, not who pays the developer.** Apple and Google still pay by their own wires (A), and Paddle or another processor pays for web sales. RevenueCat bills the developer separately for its service.
- For a Lebanon-resident individual: RevenueCat Billing and Stripe Billing need a Stripe account, which is not available (B), so **Paddle + RevenueCat** is the only combination found. Its Web Purchase Links need an invitation. Lemon Squeezy is not among RevenueCat's listed web engines, so pairing Lemon Squeezy with the app would need custom entitlement code (e.g. Lemon Squeezy webhooks → own server → RevenueCat or the app's own back end).
- The app already has a Supabase back end (project notes), so it could record web purchases itself. RevenueCat is a convenience, not a requirement.

### Gaps
- RevenueCat's pricing page could not be opened. The free threshold and percentage come from the JP page and secondary sources.

## D. Selling and receiving payouts through a company abroad (UAE free zone, US LLC/Stripe Atlas, Estonia e-Residency)

### Takeaway
A foreign company can unlock Stripe and foreign bank accounts, and a Google payments profile in that company's country. But it adds formation and yearly compliance costs (for a foreign-owned US LLC, a Form 5472 with a $25,000 penalty for missing it). Banks still check the owner's residence. Above all, **it does not move the tax**: the earlier report found that Lebanon taxes "all profits they realise in Lebanon", that is profits "generated through an effort or activity exerted in Lebanon", resident or not. Work done from Beirut therefore stays taxable in Lebanon.

### Cited Findings
**Tax caveat (from the earlier report)**
- Article 3 of the Income Tax Law (Legislative Decree 144/1959) taxes individuals and companies, "resident or not", "on all profits they realise in Lebanon". PwC reads a profit as realised in Lebanon "if it was generated through an effort or activity exerted in Lebanon". "Moving the payout account abroad does not change where the profit comes from." — `/home/user/APP/reports/Lebanon app subscription taxes.md`, section "Lebanon taxes the profit because the work happens in Lebanon", citing [Income Tax Law, Arts. 2–4 (Lebanese University legal database)](https://legallaw.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=273621&LawID=185603&language=ar) and [PwC: Lebanon corporate taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)
- A Lebanese offshore company pays a flat LBP 50 million (≈ $559) but its objectives "are limited", and the developer "would still be doing the work inside Lebanon". "Do not consider that route without a lawyer's written opinion." Interest on a foreign account is declared separately as foreign investment income. — same report, sections "A company rarely beats staying an individual" and "The Lebanese banking backdrop"

**US company (Stripe Atlas / LLC)**
- Stripe describes Atlas as helping founders "start a global business from anywhere": a Delaware company, an EIN and a US bank account. Stripe later extended it to Cuba. — [Stripe newsroom: Announcing Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/au/newsroom/news/stripe-launches-atlas); [Stripe blog: Atlas + Cuba](https://stripe.com/nz/blog/atlas-cuba) **[official, via search summary]**
- No source says Atlas excludes Lebanon. A Reddit user says Atlas bars only sanctioned jurisdictions (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea) and warns that identity checks can still block applicants from unsupported countries. Atlas incorporation does not guarantee approval for Stripe Payments. — [Reddit r/stripe: Is Stripe Atlas a legit way to use Stripe from…](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/stripe/comments/1mblgb3/is_stripe_atlas_legit_way_to_use_strip_from) **[secondary, user claim]**; [Stripe Support: Treasury availability for Atlas businesses](https://support.stripe.com/questions/treasury-account-availability-for-stripe-atlas-businesses) **[official]**
- Atlas costs $500. Atlas forms Delaware C corps per Stripe's own description; whether it now also forms LLCs is unconfirmed (one third-party review says yes). — [BusinessAnywhere vs Stripe Atlas (2026)](https://businessanywhere.io/businessanywhere-vs-stripe-atlas-better-non-us-founders/); [Tax Haven Directory: Stripe Atlas vs US LLC for non-residents](https://taxhavendirectory.com/blog/stripe-atlas-vs-us-llc-non-residents) **[secondary, vendors]**
- A foreign-owned single-member LLC must file **Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120** every year, even with no income. The penalty for not filing is **$25,000**. Whether a US LLC owes US income tax depends on effectively connected income. — [Business Today: "Forming a US LLC to access Stripe is a legitimate move. The tax compliance it creates is not small" (10 Mar 2026)](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/impact-feature/story/forming-a-us-llc-to-access-stripe-is-a-legitimate-move-the-tax-compliance-it-creates-is-not-small-519932-2026-03-10); [Rho: Delaware LLC for non-US residents, 2026 guide](https://www.rho.co/blog/delaware-llc-non-us-residents) **[secondary; Business Today item is an "impact feature" (sponsored)]**
- Lebanon has no tax treaty with the US (earlier notes, §2), so nothing reduces double taxation if the US ever taxed the LLC.

**UAE free-zone company**
- A free-zone company is not automatically tax-free. It gets 0% only on qualifying income as a "Qualifying Free Zone Person", and every company must register for UAE corporate tax. — [Uniwide: UAE free zone companies in 2026](https://www.uniwide.com/uae-free-zone-companies-in-2026-licences-tax-and-bank-accounts/); [Shanda Consult: UAE tax residency](https://shandaconsult.com/uae-residency/uae-tax-residency/) **[secondary, service providers]**
- Banks decide accounts case by case and often expect a UAE-resident signatory. Some free zones market "non-residency" set-ups with a "guaranteed" corporate bank account. — [private.law wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/uae-company-bank-account); [Gulf News (company news): Meydan Free Zone non-residency set-up](https://gulfnews.com/gn-focus/company-news/meydan-free-zone-launches-non-residency-business-setup-with-guaranteed-corporate-bank-account-1.500479010) **[secondary; Gulf News item is promotional]**
- A shareholder's home tax authority may treat the UAE company as transparent and tax its profits. — [Bestaxca](https://bestaxca.com/?p=31799) **[secondary]**
- Lebanon and the UAE have a tax treaty (earlier notes, §2). Its effect on a company managed from Lebanon was not researched.

**Estonia e-Residency**
- Officially, e-Residency "does not guarantee a business banking account", which is at the "sole discretion" of banks and fintechs. It gives no right to a personal bank account in Estonia or the EU. Some e-residents face restrictions or extra checks by citizenship. New applications are restricted for Russian and Belarusian citizens and for citizens of countries without a bilateral cooperation framework on justice and security. Digital IDs are collected only at official pick-up locations, which may need a visa. — [e-Residency: Changes in 2025 and beyond](https://e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/changes-to-e-residency-in-2025-and-beyond); [e-Residency help centre](https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-gb/articles/360000625078); [e-Residency live Q&A, Aug 2026 (PDF)](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/e-Res_liveQA_community_august_2026.pdf); [Pick-up locations](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/it/pick-up-locations/) **[official, via search summary]**
- A Lebanon-based e-resident reports a fintech account being closed and difficulty finding another. — [Nomadgate community](https://community.nomadgate.com/t/assistance-needed-opening-a-bank-account-with-estonian-e-residency/57621) **[secondary, anecdotal]**

### Inferences
- **What a foreign company changes:** access to Stripe (and so to Stripe Managed Payments, RevenueCat Billing and Polar), to bank accounts in that country, and to a Google payments profile there with a local bank. Apple and Google contracts would move to the company. That needs a D-U-N-S number and an organisation developer account (not verified here).
- **What it does not change:** the profit still comes from work done in Lebanon. Under Article 3 ("resident or not") the company itself, or the developer, can be taxed in Lebanon on it. Lebanon could also treat a company run from Beirut as effectively Lebanese. The result could be tax in two places (UAE 9% if not qualifying, US filings) with no Lebanese foreign tax credit (earlier report).
- **Banks still look through to the owner.** A UAE or US company owned by a Lebanon resident on the FATF grey list is likely to face the same enhanced checks. Some fintechs close such accounts (Nomadgate anecdote). The company solves Stripe's country rule, not the compliance friction.
- **Cost against benefit for a small developer:** the earlier report found a Lebanese company "rarely beats staying an individual". A foreign company adds formation fees ($500 Atlas, UAE licence), yearly filings and accountants on top of continuing Lebanese obligations. That is justified only if web revenue needs Stripe specifically, and only with a written Lebanese tax opinion.

### Gaps

- UAE banks' 2026 policy toward Lebanese-resident shareholders, actual UAE free-zone costs, and how the Lebanon–UAE treaty treats a company effectively managed from Lebanon were not researched.
- No Lebanese MoF guidance on foreign companies owned and run by Lebanese residents (controlled foreign company rules or place of management) was found.

## Store rules for selling on the web alongside store apps, and practical recommendation inputs

### Takeaway
On Apple, telling users about web purchases or linking to them is allowed **only on the US storefront** (since May 2025). Elsewhere, Apple only permits the 3.1.3(b) "multiplatform" model: a subscription bought on the web unlocks the app, without steering users to it. Google's external-offers rules were not found in this session (another researcher covers them). For a small Lebanese developer, the simplest safe combination is: **store billing paid into a fresh USD Lebanese account + optionally a web version sold through an MoR that pays to Lebanon (Lemon Squeezy, or Paddle once confirmed) + no foreign company.**

### Cited Findings
- In May 2025, after a US court order, Apple changed its guidelines. On the US storefront developers no longer need special entitlements to include "buttons, links, or other prompts directing users to external websites for purchases". The rule against encouraging other purchase methods "does not apply on the United States storefront". — [MacRumors (1 May 2025)](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/05/01/apple-updates-u-s-app-review-guidelines-epic/); [Daring Fireball (2 May 2025)](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/05/02/updated-app-review-guidelines-us); [iClarified (5 Dec 2025)](https://www.iclarified.com/97192/apple-updates-app-store-rules-to-allow-external-purchase-links-in-us) **[secondary, news]**
- Guideline 3.1.3(b) (multiplatform) lets apps "allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site". Outside the US, external purchase links remain prohibited unless an entitlement applies. — [ConductAtlas: anti-steering / external purchase link provisions](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/provision/CA-P-018912/external-purchase-link-entitlement-required-outside-us/); [newly.app: App Store external purchase link (updated Oct 2026)](https://newly.app/how-to/app-store-external-purchase-link) **[secondary]**
- The US position is still being litigated (contempt/compliance proceedings). — [Mondaq: Court finds Apple violated order…](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/consumer-law/1621598/court-finds-apple-violated-order-resulting-in-key-changes-for-ios-external-purchase-methods) **[secondary, law-firm article]**
- Google Play external offers / user choice billing: no result in this session.
- Recommendation inputs from A–D: Google requires a bank in the profile country ([Play Console Help 7161426](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161426?hl=en)). Lemon Squeezy lists Lebanon for bank payouts ([Lemon Squeezy Docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)). Paddle's visible seller exclusions do not include Lebanon ([Paddle Help](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle)). Stripe is not available to Lebanon sellers per community reports ([Adalo forum](https://forum.adalo.com/t/stripe-payments-country-not-supported/24648)). The RevenueCat Paddle web links are invite-only ([RevenueCat Docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/web-billing/overview)). **[all via search summaries]**

- **Simplest and safest for a small developer:** (1) keep store billing as the main channel, paid into a **fresh USD account at a Lebanese bank in the developer's own name**, swept regularly (earlier report). (2) If a web version is wanted, sell it through an **MoR**, because a Lebanon-based individual cannot open Stripe or receive PayPal, and an MoR also removes foreign VAT registration. Confirm Lemon Squeezy's Lebanon bank payout and Paddle's seller acceptance in writing first. (3) Link to web purchase only on the US App Store storefront. Elsewhere use the multiplatform model (no steering). Check Google's current external-offers terms with the store-rules researcher. (4) Avoid fintech accounts that do not officially serve Lebanese residents, and avoid foreign companies unless Stripe becomes essential and a Lebanese tax adviser confirms in writing.
