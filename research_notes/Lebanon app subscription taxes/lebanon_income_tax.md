## 1. Which part of the Income Tax Law applies, and is an app developer a "non-commercial profession" or a commercial activity?

### Takeaway
The income is taxed under Title I of the Income Tax Law, which covers profits of industrial, commercial and non-commercial professions. Lebanon taxes each type of income separately: a person's salaries and business profits are never added together into one overall tax. The Ministry of Finance (MoF) defines liberal ("non-commercial") professions broadly enough to include an independent developer. But no source was found that says how selling app subscriptions specifically is classified: as a liberal profession (intellectual work) or as a commercial activity (selling digital products). In practice the regime and the rates are the same for an individual either way (see sections 2 and 3). The classification mainly matters for registering with the Commercial Register, and possibly for the lump-sum percentage that applies.

### Cited Findings
- Lebanese income tax is assessed by type of income. The law does not have a "global" tax on total income, so there is no single progressive tax over all sources combined. — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Lebanon – Individual – Taxes on personal income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/taxes-on-personal-income) (page undated in extract; references 2024 and 2026 budget laws)
- Article 46 of the 2024 Budget Law applies the amended progressive brackets to "taxable income generated by industrial, commercial and non-commercial entities and individuals", starting 1 January 2024. — [ALDIC, Summary of the Law 324 Budget 2024 tax amendments (June 2024)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summary-of-the-Law-324-Budget-2024-Tax-amendments.pdf); heading "Article 46 – Tax on profits of commercial, industrial, and non-commercial professions" also in [MoF, Citizen Budget 2024 (ENG)](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Finance/BI/ABDP/Annual%20Budget%20Documents%20and%20Process/Citizen%20Budget%202024-ENG.pdf)
- The MoF's guide for self-employed (liberal) professionals defines a free profession as "every profession that generates for its practitioner a non-commercial, non-industrial income or handicraft income". — [MoF, The Income Tax Guide for Self-Employed (Liberal) Professionals](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/IncomeTax%20Guide.pdf) (dated 2003 per search extract: old, check for updates)
- "Any person working as a self-employed person in Lebanon (Lebanese and foreigner) must abide by Lebanese tax laws and thus register with the Tax Administration." — [NRC, Lebanon Self-Employment and Small Business Guide](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/guidelines/guide-to-self-employment-and-small-business/lebanon-self-employment-and-small-business-guide_en.pdf) (secondary, NGO guide)
- A 2016 non-resident tax memo lists "sale of IT programs" as a separate category of revenue (taxed at 2.25% when paid to non-residents under Decree 3692 of 22 June 2016). So Lebanese tax practice treats selling software as a sale, alongside software as a service. — [ALDIC, Tax memo on Decree No. 3692 (2016/2017)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Decree-3692-dated-22-June-2016-on-non-resident-Bilingual-.pdf) (old: non-resident rates were raised in 2024, see section 4)

### Inferences
- Custom development or consulting done for clients fits the MoF's "liberal profession" definition well. Publishing apps and selling subscriptions to the public through stores looks more like a sale of products or licences, which could be treated as commercial (as the "sale of IT programs" category suggests). Both fall under Title I with the same progressive scale for an individual. So the tax rate does not change, but the choice of regime and the Commercial Register question may.
- Because there is no overall income tax, income from a salaried job held at the same time would be taxed separately under the salaries schedule (Title II), with its own brackets.

### Gaps
- No MoF circular or ruling was found that classifies app publishing or software subscriptions as commercial or non-commercial. A Lebanese tax adviser, or a written question to the MoF, should confirm this.
- I could not confirm the article of the Income Tax Law that lists the non-commercial professions (believed to be Article 1). The full text of Decree 144/1959 could not be read.

## 2. Assessment regimes (real profit / lump-sum / estimated profit): criteria and which one applies to an individual developer

### Takeaway
There are traditionally three methods:
- Real profit (الربح الحقيقي): profit from the accounts, with books kept.
- Lump-sum profit (الربح المقطوع): taxable profit is a percentage of turnover, set by the MoF for each activity.
- Estimated profit (الربح المقدّر): the MoF fixes the profit itself, and the taxpayer files no return.

A major change took effect on 1 January 2026. Under MoF Notification No. 4913 of 24 Dec 2025, taxpayers on the field-assessed method move to the lump-sum method and must register and file online. Each source uses its own English names for the methods; the Arabic terms and the descriptions are what tell them apart. As of November 2026, an individual developer therefore effectively chooses between:
- lump-sum: the default for most liberal professions; a return is due before 1 February;
- real profit: by option (asked for before the end of January, cannot be undone) or when required; a return is due before 1 April.
No current turnover threshold that makes real profit compulsory for an individual was found.

### Cited Findings
- Three methods are used to determine taxable profit: real profit, lump-sum profit and estimated profit. — [Lexology, In review: direct taxation of businesses in Lebanon](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=4e046925-b05b-4445-a3d5-63818619adbb) (secondary, date not shown in extract)
- Real profit is mandatory for certain entities (mainly joint-stock and limited liability companies). Small entities may opt into it voluntarily, and once they do they cannot go back. Under lump-sum, taxable income is a percentage of sales, set each year by the MoF from the sales figures the taxpayer reports. Under the "deemed" (field-assessed) method, the MoF fixes taxable income directly after a field audit and the taxpayer files no return. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)
- **2026 change:** MoF Notification No. 4913 of 24 December 2025 states that taxpayers taxed under the deemed (field-assessed) profit method move to the lump-sum method from 1 January 2026. They must register and file their returns through the MoF's online portal. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); same change reported by [Balance Brightness blog, "Income Tax Lebanon 2026"](https://balancebrightness.com/blog/income-tax-lebanon) (low-authority blog)
- The lump-sum regime is applied by default to most free professions. It is calculated by applying the tax rate to a percentage of turnover, and that percentage is set by a Minister of Finance decision for each class of activity. Everyone else falls under the estimated profits regime (bénéfice estimé). — [IMF Country Report No. 23/8, Lebanon: Technical Assistance Report on Putting Tax Policy Back on Track (Jan 2023)](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2023/english/1lbnea2023001.pdf)
- (Arabic) Liberal professions are usually taxed on real profit or lump-sum profit. The taxpayer may ask to be taxed on real profit by a request made before the end of January of the tax year, and the choice cannot be reversed. Lump-sum taxpayers file before 1 February (Art. 17 of the Income Tax Law). — [Kallas Law Firm, ضريبة الدخل والإجراءات الضريبية في لبنان: دليل عملي وفق النصوص النافذة حتى موازنة 2026](https://www.klfi.net/lebanon-tax-guide-income-tax-procedures/) (secondary, 2026)
- (Arabic, MoF page) Lump-sum taxable profit is found by applying an average annual rate that the lump-sum committee sets for each type of profession. The tax is then applied to that profit. The same page gives a 15% rate, which is the old pre-2017 company rate, so the page is outdated. — [MoF, احتساب الضريبة](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Companies/TOC-NCP/CTD/Pages/Tax-Calculation.aspx) (outdated page)
- One guide gives a lump-sum taxable profit of 50% of annual income for certain professions. The extract does not say which professions. — search extract attributed to [MoF, Income Tax Guide for Self-Employed (Liberal) Professionals](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/IncomeTax%20Guide.pdf) (2003, unconfirmed)
- From the end of 2023 until 31 Dec 2026, a 2024 amendment lets lump-sum-method taxpayers revalue their fixed assets every year instead of every five years. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)

### Inferences
- From 2026 a solo developer is, by default, on lump-sum: taxable profit = turnover × the MoF's profit percentage for the activity, then the progressive scale (section 3). The developer may opt for real profit before the end of January of the tax year. That makes sense when the real margin is below the MoF percentage (for example with large store commissions, ad spend or outside contractors), or when proper accounts are wanted for later incorporation or bank purposes. The option is irreversible.
- Store commissions (15–30%) are taken before Apple or Google pay the developer. Under lump-sum, it matters a great deal whether "turnover" means the price paid by customers or the net proceeds received. Under real profit, this question goes away because gross sales minus commission gives the same result as net proceeds.

### Gaps
- No current MoF lump-sum percentage was found for software development, app publishing or "IT programs" activities. These come from the lump-sum committee's decisions, which were not found online.
- No current statutory turnover threshold was found that forces an individual onto real profit (or bars lump-sum). The text of Notification 4913 was not obtained either.
- Whether lump-sum turnover for app-store sales is gross customer price or net proceeds is not addressed by any source found.

## 3. Progressive brackets and rates (LBP, after the 2024 budget), family deductions, and how they apply

### Takeaway
Since 1 January 2024 (Law 324/2024, Art. 46), Title I profits of individuals are taxed in progressive bands:
- 4% up to LBP 540 million
- 7% from LBP 540 million to 1.44 billion
- 12% from LBP 1.44 billion to 3.24 billion
- 16% from LBP 3.24 billion to 6.24 billion
- 21% from LBP 6.24 billion to 13.5 billion
- 25% above LBP 13.5 billion

At 89,500 LBP per USD, the bands end at about $6.0k, $16.1k, $36.2k, $69.7k and $150.8k.

The family deductions are subtracted before the scale is applied: LBP 450 million for the taxpayer, LBP 225 million for a non-working spouse, and LBP 45 million per dependent child (up to five). No source reports that the 2025 or 2026 budgets changed the brackets or the deductions. Companies pay a flat 17%.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 profit bands: 4% up to LBP 540m; 7% up to LBP 1.44bn; 12% up to LBP 3.24bn; 16% up to LBP 6.24bn (the extract stops there). The rates themselves are unchanged; only the bands were raised, applicable from 1 Jan 2024. — [ALDIC, Summary of the Law 324 Budget 2024 tax amendments (June 2024)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summary-of-the-Law-324-Budget-2024-Tax-amendments.pdf)
- Top bands: 21% for profits between LBP 6.24 bn and 13.5 bn, and 25% above LBP 13.5 bn, under "Article 46 – Tax on profits of commercial, industrial, and non-commercial professions". The document writes some amounts as "trillion", so its scale is inconsistent. — [MoF, Citizen Budget 2024](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Finance/BI/ABDP/Annual%20Budget%20Documents%20and%20Process/Citizen%20Budget%202024-ENG.pdf); the same 16% (3.24–6.24 bn) / 21% (6.24–13.5 bn) / 25% (>13.5 bn) bands appear in [This is Beirut, "Tax Figures and Percentages" (2024 budget)](https://thisisbeirut.com.lb/articles/1225827/taxes-and-fees-2024-budget) and [Moore Global, Lebanon Tax Guide](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/)
- PwC: the 2024 Budget Law raised the profit brackets to a range of LL 540 million to LL 13.5 billion, with rates from 4% to 25%. — [PwC, Lebanon – Individual – Taxes on personal income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)
- The 4–25% scale is in Article 32 of the Income Tax Law. Family deductions are in Articles 31 and 56: LBP 450m for the taxpayer, 225m for the spouse, 45m per child, up to 5 children. — [Lebanese Legislation, "Income tax brackets: Decree-Law 144 of 1959"](https://lebaneselegislation.com/en/topic/income-tax-brackets/) (secondary legal database). PwC instead attributes the 4–25% range to Art. 24 of the 2019 Budget Law, which amended Art. 32 — per search extract of [PwC individual pages](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)
- Family deductions under the 2024 budget: LBP 450 million for the individual taxpayer, LBP 225 million for a non-working spouse, LBP 45 million per dependent child up to five (with conditions). — [MoF, Citizen Budget 2024](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Finance/BI/ABDP/Annual%20Budget%20Documents%20and%20Process/Citizen%20Budget%202024-ENG.pdf); same figures, effective 1 Jan 2024, in [ALDIC Tax Alert: tax on salaries according to the 2024 Budget Law](https://www.aldic.net/tax-alert-tax-on-salaries-according-to-the-budget-law-of-the-year-2024/)
- **Conflict on 2024 timing:** L'Orient Today reports that the 2024 budget only took effect in mid-February 2024 and that the MoF pro-rated the amounts for that year (married deduction LL 198,437,500; child deduction LL 39,687,500). — [L'Orient Today, "Finance Ministry announces two new income tax features"](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1453786/finance-ministry-announces-two-new-income-tax-features.html) (article date not confirmed). Relevant only to FY2024. For 2025 and 2026, the full-year figures should apply unless a later law changed them.
- **Conflict on salary bands:** ALDIC's salaries alert gives a salary scale starting at 2% up to LBP 360 million. This differs from the profit scale; it is a different schedule (Title II). — [ALDIC Tax Alert (2024)](https://www.aldic.net/tax-alert-tax-on-salaries-according-to-the-budget-law-of-the-year-2024/)
- Companies (SAL/SARL) pay a flat 17% corporate income tax. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [Lexology, direct taxation of businesses](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=4e046925-b05b-4445-a3d5-63818619adbb)
- Older and outdated secondary sources still give 4–21% for business income (a pre-2019 scale), e.g. [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=7f07b3e7-d203-460e-89b4-8c1da11d7373). Ignore these.
- 2025 budget: issued by Decree No. 56 of 11 March 2025 and published in the Official Gazette on 13 March 2025. The Finance Minister was asked to draft a law revising taxes and fees. One outlet says the budget did not change the brackets. — [Byblos Bank, Lebanon This Week No. 880](https://byblos.byblosbank.com/files/Library/Assets/Gallery/Publications/LebanonThisWeek/Lebanon%20This%20Week%20880.pdf); [MoF, Citizen Budget 2025](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Finance/BI/ABDP/Annual%20Budget%20Documents%20and%20Process/Citizen%20Budget%202025%20en.pdf)
- 2026 budget (Law No. 40, published 10 Feb 2026, in force on publication). The income-tax measures reported are:
  - a 1.5% advance tax collected at customs from importers who did not file income tax or VAT returns in the previous 3 years;
  - a 17% "exceptional" tax on Sayrafa profits above USD 100,000 (salaries exempted);
  - fixed and minimum fines under the Tax Procedures Law multiplied by 25 from FY2026;
  - shorter deadlines and higher fines for beneficial-owner (UBO) reporting.
  No change to the individual profit brackets is reported. — [Orbitax, "Lebanon Budget 2026 Measures…"](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851); [Phoenix Law Firm, "Lebanon's 2026 Budget Law: the tax, fee and fine changes"](https://phoenix-lawfirm.com/lebanon-2026-budget-law-tax-fee-fine-changes/); [ALDIC, Summary of the Law 40 Budget 2026 tax amendments (March 2026)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Summary-of-the-Law-40-Budget-2026-Tax-amendments.pdf); [Credit Libanais, "Parliament ratifies 2026 budget law"](https://economics.creditlibanais.com/Article/213139); [PwC, Lebanon – Individual – Significant developments](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/significant-developments) (2026 Budget Law issued 10 Feb 2026)

### Inferences
- Worked example under real profit (or lump-sum profit of the same amount), single taxpayer, converted at 89,500 LBP/USD:
  - **Net profit USD 30,000.** That is LBP 2.685 bn; minus the 450m deduction leaves LBP 2.235 bn taxable. Tax = 4%×540m (21.6m) + 7%×900m (63m) + 12%×795m (95.4m) = LBP 180.0m ≈ **USD 2,011**, about 6.7% of profit.
  - **Net profit USD 100,000.** That is LBP 8.95 bn; minus 450m leaves 8.5 bn taxable. Tax = 21.6m + 63m + 216m + 480m + 21%×2.26bn (474.6m) = LBP 1,255.2m ≈ **USD 14,025**, about 14%.
  - A non-working spouse adds LBP 225m (≈ $2.5k) of deduction, and each child LBP 45m (≈ $0.5k).
- The bands were set in LBP in 2024 and are not indexed. If the official rate stays at 89,500, they stay at the USD values above.
- Under lump-sum, the tax base is turnover × the MoF percentage instead of real profit. With, say, a 50% percentage (the figure is unverified, see section 2), USD 60k of turnover would be taxed like USD 30k of profit.

### Gaps
- The official Gazette text of Law 324 Art. 46 could not be read. The bands come from the MoF Citizen Budget and secondary sources, which agree on the figures but cite different article numbers.
- No source says whether Law 40/2026 or any later law in 2026 changed the brackets or family deductions. None of the sources read reports a change.

## 4. Territoriality: income from foreign customers paid by Apple/Google to a developer working in Lebanon; residents' foreign-source income

### Takeaway
Lebanon taxes business profits on a territorial basis, according to where the activity is carried out. Profits from an activity performed in Lebanon are taxable even when the customers and the paying company (Apple, Google) are abroad. A developer who builds and runs the apps from Lebanon should therefore treat the subscription income as taxable under Title I. Only business carried out abroad through a foreign branch or agent is outside the scope. The 2024 budget did not bring in worldwide taxation of residents' business income; Lebanon still has no global income tax. Residents are already taxed on foreign investment income (Title III, 10%). Law 324 also requires taxes on income received in foreign currency to be paid in that currency (section 6).

### Cited Findings
- Business transactions carried out abroad through agents or branches are normally not subject to Lebanese business income tax. Personal income tax applies when the services that triggered the income are performed on Lebanese territory, even if the money comes from outside Lebanon. — [HBD-T / ALDIC, "How to do business in Lebanon – legal & fiscal framework" (updated 2024)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/HOW-TO-DO-BUSINESS-IN-LEBANON-LEGAL-FISCAL-FRAMWORK-FOR-INVESTMENTS-updated-2024-Summary-presentation-ALDIC.pdf)
- PwC (salaries): tax applies when the services that triggered the income are carried out on Lebanese territory, or when the funding source is in Lebanon. — [PwC, Lebanon – Individual – Taxes on personal income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)
- One guide treats profits made outside Lebanon as taxable when they come from an activity carried out in Lebanon. — [PwC, Doing Business in Lebanon (tax and legal guide)](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guide-lebanon.pdf) (as summarised in a search extract; guide date not confirmed)
- Movable capital income (interest, dividends, bonds, etc.) is taxable whether it arises in Lebanon or is received from abroad by a Lebanese resident. — [Global Legal Post, Private client law guide – Lebanon](https://www.globallegalpost.com/lawoverborders/private-client-law-guide-977924288/lebanon-1212379153); [IBA, Lebanon International Estate Planning Guide](https://www.ibanet.org/document?id=Lebanon-international-estate-planning). Income from foreign shares and bonds is taxed at 10% — [Lloyds Bank Trade, Tax rates in Lebanon](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/lebanon/taxes)
- Since 15 November 2022, tax on income from foreign shares, bonds and other foreign movable assets must be paid in foreign currency. — [PwC, Doing Business in Lebanon 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbilb.pdf)
- The MoF's Executive Budget Proposal 2023–2024 described a 2% tax on revenue generated in Lebanon by any natural or legal person working on activities outside Lebanon, such as money transfers or **services provided online**, collected in the same currency as the revenue. — [MoF/Institut des Finances, Citizen Budget – Executive's Budget Proposal 2023–2024](https://www.institutdesfinances.gov.lb/sites/default/files/2024-05/Citizen%20Budget%20-%20Executive's%20Budget%20Proposal%202023-2024-ENG-Final.pdf). **This was a proposal.** No source confirms it is in Law 324/2024 as enacted.
- Non-resident tax under the 2024 budget: 3.4% on goods and 8.5% on services (50% of the 17% corporate rate); per the Citizen Budget, 20% of receipts for money transfers and 50% for services are taxed at the flat rate. — [ALDIC, Summary of Law 324](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summary-of-the-Law-324-Budget-2024-Tax-amendments.pdf); [MoF, Citizen Budget 2024](https://www.institutdesfinances.gov.lb/sites/default/files/2024-10/Citizen%20Budget%202024-ENG_1.pdf)
- Lebanese freelancer guides (low authority) say freelancers must declare all their income even when it is received in foreign currency. — [Tomohi, Freelancer pricing guide Lebanon](https://tomohi.com/freelancer-pricing-guide-lebanon/); [Beaumont Capital Markets, Financial strategies for Lebanese freelancers 2026](https://beaumont-capitalmarkets.co.uk/essential-financial-strategies-for-lebanese-freelancers-2026/)

### Inferences
- Taken together, the sources point to the same answer: the developer performs the work in Lebanon, so the profit is Lebanese-source Title I profit, even though Apple and Google pay from abroad. No Lebanese withholding applies to what Apple or Google pay, so the developer must self-declare.
- Withholding when the developer pays non-residents: the non-resident tax (8.5% on services, 3.4% on goods) is normally withheld by the Lebanese payer. In principle, a Lebanese taxpayer paying foreign providers for services (cloud hosting, contractors, possibly the Apple and Google developer fees) may have to withhold and pay it. Practice for small payments to large platforms was not found. This needs an adviser's check.
- If the developer holds foreign bank or brokerage accounts that earn interest or dividends, that income is separately taxable at 10% under Title III, payable in foreign currency.

### Gaps
- No official MoF ruling was found on remote freelancers or app publishers with only foreign customers.
- I could not confirm whether the proposed 2% tax on revenue from activities outside Lebanon or online services was dropped, enacted, or reproposed in 2025 or 2026.
- Tax treaties and credits for any foreign withholding are not covered. Apple and Google do not usually withhold on Lebanese developers, but this was not researched.

## 5. Registration: tax number, forms, where, online portal, Commercial Register

### Takeaway
A self-employed person starting an activity must register with the MoF's income tax department and obtain a tax number (رقم مالي). The MoF issues one tax identification number that covers all taxes. Registration was traditionally done at the department in Beirut or in the governorate where the activity takes place. Since the 2026 shift to lump-sum, registration and filing for these taxpayers are meant to go through the MoF online portal (eservices.finance.gov.lb). The form numbers could not be confirmed (one blog says M10/M11). Commercial Register registration (Ministry of Justice) is a separate step that applies to merchants and companies. No source confirms whether an individual app publisher needs it.

### Cited Findings
- A self-employed person starting a profession or business in Lebanon for the first time must make a declaration to the Income Tax Department in Beirut, or in the governorate where the activity takes place. The guide says a professional starting out must register before 1 February of the tax year and must keep the registration receipt or certificate. — [MoF, Income Tax Guide for Self-Employed (Liberal) Professionals](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/IncomeTax%20Guide.pdf) (2003 guide; deadlines and forms may be outdated)
- From 1 Jan 2026, taxpayers moved from the field-assessed method to lump-sum must register and file through the MoF's online portal (Notification 4913, 24 Dec 2025). — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)
- The MoF e-taxation system ("Daribati", eservices.finance.gov.lb) provides registration information and online submission of tax forms. — [Lebweb listing, Ministry of Finance – Daribati – E-Taxation System](http://www.lebweb.com/site/lebanon-eservices-finance-gov-lb-143975) (directory listing)
- The TIN is issued by the MoF's Revenue Directorate, and a single tax number covers income tax, VAT and other taxes. — [LookupTax, Lebanon TIN guide](https://lookuptax.com/docs/tax-identification-number/lebanon-tax-id-guide); [TaxDo, Lebanon TIN guide](https://taxdo.com/resources/global-tax-id-validation-guide/lebanon) (both low-authority aggregators; TaxDo's claim of an online application and verification tool is unconfirmed)
- A blog says freelancers should register with the MoF within two months of starting, on forms M10/M11, and that many work informally at first. — [Tomohi, Freelancer pricing guide Lebanon](https://tomohi.com/freelancer-pricing-guide-lebanon/) (single low-authority source; form numbers unverified; conflicts with the 2003 MoF guide's "before 1 February")
- The Commercial Register is run by the Ministry of Justice (cr.justice.gov.lb). After the register certificate, a business registers with the MoF for a tax number. — [leb.business, "How to register a company in Lebanon (2026)"](https://leb.business/en/article/how-to-register-a-company-in-lebanon-a-complete-step-by-step-guide-2026) (company-focused, secondary)
- VAT interaction: registration is compulsory above about USD 55,900 of turnover, per a guide citing the 2024 budget law. — [Productive Levant, Lebanon VAT number registration](https://productivelevant.com/en/lebanon-vat-number-registration/) (secondary; VAT is covered by another researcher)

### Inferences
- Practical route as of November 2026: obtain a tax number from the MoF (online portal or tax office), declare the activity and regime, and, if real profit is wanted, file the option request before the end of January of the tax year.
- If the activity is classed as a liberal profession, Commercial Register registration should not be needed. If it is classed as commercial (an individual merchant), it may be. This is not confirmed (section 1).

### Gaps
- The current registration form names and numbers (M1/M2 vs M10/M11) and the current registration deadline could not be verified.
- No official step-by-step guide for registering online as an individual in 2026 was found.

## 6. Filing: forms, deadlines, currency, exchange rate, payment in USD

### Takeaway
- **Deadlines:** lump-sum taxpayers file the annual return before 1 February of the following year. Real-profit individuals file before 1 April (31 March). Companies file by 31 May (before 1 June). The MoF often extends deadlines: a reported extension moved lump-sum/"deemed" FY2025 returns to 27 Feb 2026, and Law No. 46 of 27 July 2026 suspended some deadlines.
- **Currency:** returns and tax are computed in LBP. Foreign-currency amounts are converted at the central bank (BDL) official rate of 89,500 LBP/USD, in use since early 2024.
- **Payment in USD:** Law 324/2024 requires taxes on revenue received in foreign currency to be paid in that same currency. So tax on USD app income may be payable in "fresh" dollars. How this works for Title I profit returns is not documented in the sources.

### Cited Findings
- Real-profit individuals: return due before 1 April of the following year. Lump-sum self-employed professionals: before 1 February. — [MoF, Income Tax Guide for Self-Employed (Liberal) Professionals](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/IncomeTax%20Guide.pdf) (2003); [PwC, Lebanon – Individual – Tax administration](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/tax-administration); Moore gives 31 March for individuals and 31 May for companies — [Moore Global, Lebanon Tax Guide](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/). TaxAtlas (an outlier) gives 31 May for business income — [TaxAtlas](https://taxatlas.io/country/lebanon) (low authority).
- (Arabic) Lump-sum taxpayers file before 1 February under Art. 17 of the Income Tax Law. — [Kallas Law Firm guide (2026)](https://www.klfi.net/lebanon-tax-guide-income-tax-procedures/)
- MoF Decision #57/1 reportedly extended the FY2025 filing deadline for "deemed-profit" (lump-sum) taxpayers to 27 Feb 2026. — [ALDIC, Notifications & Regulations](https://www.aldic.net/category/notifications-regulations/) (not verified against the decision itself)
- The MoF referred to Law No. 46 of 27 July 2026 on suspending deadlines, and extended an October 2026 declaration deadline to Monday 2 Nov 2026. That extension concerns banks and financial institutions. — [Daily Beirut, "Finance Ministry extends tax declaration deadline to November 2"](https://dailybeirut.com/en/lebanon-news/ministry-of-finance-extends-tax-declaration-deadline-to-november-2/)
- Law 324/2024: taxes, duties and penalties due in foreign currency are paid in LBP at the real value set by the central bank, **except** amounts owed to the State in foreign currency and "taxes and fees on revenues, interests, and proceeds received in foreign currencies". The MoF review summarises this as "taxes on foreign revenues to be paid in the same currency in which the revenues were earned (Art. 41)". Tax fines on foreign-currency taxes are paid in the same currency. — [MoF, Public Finance Annual Review 2024](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/Annual%20Public%20Finance%20Monitor%202024.pdf); [MoF, Citizen Budget 2024](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Finance/BI/ABDP/Annual%20Budget%20Documents%20and%20Process/Citizen%20Budget%202024-ENG.pdf). "Art. 41" here is the budget law's numbering, not Art. 41 of the Income Tax Law (which deals with non-residents).
- The BDL platform rate has been 89,500 LBP/USD since late 2023 (19 Dec 2023 per the source). — [Lexology, "Year in review: inward investment and international taxation in Lebanon"](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b0e2d5cd-1f1f-49e6-b3eb-ed083f0035a2). Decree No. 302 of 3 April 2024 sets conversion at 89,500 LBP/USD from 1 April 2024 (for salaries) — [CRS, News flash 11 Oct 2024, Lebanon updated tax brackets, family exemptions and exchange rate](https://www.crs.co.za/news-flash-11-october-2024-lebanon-updated-tax-brackets-family-exemption-limits-and-exchange-rate/)
- 2025 Citizen Budget: income tax on salaries paid wholly or partly in foreign currency is computed in LBP at the central bank's official rate. — [MoF, Citizen Budget 2025](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Finance/BI/ABDP/Annual%20Budget%20Documents%20and%20Process/Citizen%20Budget%202025%20en.pdf)
- A blog names the annual business and professional return "R5" and the payroll return "R7". — [Balance Brightness, "Income Tax Lebanon 2026"](https://balancebrightness.com/blog/income-tax-lebanon) (unofficial; unverified)

### Inferences
- For FY2026 (the first year of the lump-sum shift): a lump-sum return is due before 1 Feb 2027; a real-profit return before 1 Apr 2027. Watch for the usual MoF extensions.
- USD income should be converted at 89,500, the BDL official rate for the year, and the bands applied in LBP. Because of the Law 324 rule on revenues "received in foreign currencies", the MoF may require the tax on USD store proceeds to be paid in USD. Confirm with the MoF or an adviser before filing.
- The developer should keep monthly Apple/Google payout reports in USD as evidence of the amounts converted.

### Gaps
- The current official form codes for Title I returns of individuals (lump-sum and real profit) were not verified.
- No MoF circular was found on how the "pay in the currency earned" rule applies to Title I profits of individuals with USD income. Nor was the exact rate to use for profit returns found (year-end rate vs average vs transaction-date rate).

## 7. Bookkeeping and deductible expenses (store commissions, developer fees, equipment)

### Takeaway
Real-profit liberal professionals must keep an income and expenditure journal and a fixed-assets register. Expenses necessary for the activity are then deductible: store commissions, the Apple/Google developer programme fees, hosting and software subscriptions, contractors, and depreciation of computers. Lump-sum taxpayers deduct nothing, because their taxable profit is a fixed percentage of turnover; they mainly need records of turnover. No source specific to software or app-store expenses was found.

### Cited Findings
- Liberal professionals must file a declaration, keep the registration receipt or certificate, and keep an income and expenditure journal and a fixed-assets register. — [MoF, Income Tax Guide for Self-Employed (Liberal) Professionals](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/IncomeTax%20Guide.pdf) (2003)
- Lump-sum taxable income is a percentage of sales reported by the taxpayer. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [IMF CR 23/8 (2023)](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2023/english/1lbnea2023001.pdf)
- Companies on real profit are taxed on accounting profit after tax adjustments. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)

### Inferences
- Under real profit, gross store sales minus the Apple/Google commission (15–30%) should equal net proceeds. Other costs are deductible if they are documented and necessary for the activity. This follows the general Lebanese real-profit principle; the specific article was not read.
- Payments to foreign providers may carry a non-resident withholding duty (section 4). Unpaid withholding could make the expense non-deductible or bring penalties. This needs an adviser's check.

### Gaps
- The text of the Income Tax Law article on deductible expenses (believed to be Art. 7) and current depreciation rates were not obtained.
- I found no guidance on whether record-keeping or e-invoicing obligations changed in 2026 for lump-sum taxpayers filing online.

## 8. Penalties for non-registration and late filing

### Takeaway
Under the Tax Procedures Law (Law No. 44 of 2008), as reported by secondary sources:
- **Late filing:** 5% of the tax per month of delay, capped at 100%. A VAT-focused source says the 2024 budget raised this to 10% per month and set fixed minimum fines.
- **Late payment:** about 1% per month.
- **Under-declared tax:** 20% of the difference.
- **Not registered:** a liberal professional who does not register is assessed directly by the MoF and can be fined 100% of the tax.

The 2026 budget multiplies all fixed and minimum fines by 25 from FY2026. A settlement scheme reducing penalties on assessments issued since 16 Nov 2022 runs until 30 Dec 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Late filing: 5% per month, capped at 100%. Adjustment of a return: 20% of the difference between net tax owed and net tax due. Late payment: 1% per month (1.5% for withholding taxes and VAT). — [Moore Global, Lebanon Tax Guide](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/) (secondary; date not shown)
- **Conflict:** a VAT-focused guide says the 2024 Budget Law raised the late-filing penalty to 10% of the tax per month, capped at 100%. It gives minimums of LBP 6,750,000 for joint-stock companies and LBP 4,500,000 for other companies. — [Kintsugi, Lebanon VAT Guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon) (secondary, about VAT; applicability to income tax not confirmed)
- A self-employed (liberal) professional who does not comply is assessed directly and is liable to a fine of 100% of the tax. — [NRC, Lebanon Self-Employment and Small Business Guide](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/guidelines/guide-to-self-employment-and-small-business/lebanon-self-employment-and-small-business-guide_en.pdf); same rule in [MoF, Income Tax Guide for Self-Employed (Liberal) Professionals](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Our_Latest/Latest%20News/English/IncomeTax%20Guide.pdf) (2003)
- 2026 budget (Law 40): fixed and minimum fines under the Tax Procedures Law are raised 25-fold from FY2026. — [Orbitax, Lebanon Budget 2026 measures](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851); [Phoenix Law Firm (2026)](https://phoenix-lawfirm.com/lebanon-2026-budget-law-tax-fee-fine-changes/)
- 2026 budget: customs collects a 1.5% advance on income tax from importers who missed income tax or VAT filings in the previous three years. — [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851)
- Penalty settlement: for assessments issued from 16 November 2022, settlement is open until 30 December 2026, with conditions including payment. Reductions are 85% for proportional verification penalties, 60% for flat-rate verification penalties and 75% for collection penalties. Penalties on VAT and salary-tax self-assessments are not reduced. — [RegFollower, "Lebanon grants tax penalty reductions on eligible assessments until end-2026"](https://regfollower.com/lebanon-grants-tax-penalty-reductions-on-eligible-assessments-until-end-2026/). An earlier MoF settlement, Decision 768/1 of 3 Sep 2025, reduced income-tax penalties until 30 Sep 2025 (expired) — reported in the same search as [Byblos Bank, Lebanon This Week No. 880](https://byblos.byblosbank.com/files/Library/Assets/Gallery/Publications/LebanonThisWeek/Lebanon%20This%20Week%20880.pdf)
- Filing within an officially published extension carries no late-filing penalty, though interest still runs on any unpaid tax. — search extract of [Lebanon VAT number guide](https://productivelevant.com/en/lebanon-vat-number-registration/) / [Daily Beirut](https://dailybeirut.com/en/lebanon-news/ministry-of-finance-extends-tax-declaration-deadline-to-november-2/) (attribution within the extract uncertain)

### Inferences
- A developer who has earned app income for some years without registering faces direct assessment, a late-filing penalty of up to 100% of the tax, and late-payment interest. From FY2026, minimum fines are much higher (×25). If they qualify, using the 2026 settlement window (until 30 Dec 2026) could reduce penalties on assessments already issued.

### Gaps
- No primary text of the current Tax Procedures Law penalty articles (as amended by Laws 324/2024 and 40/2026) was read. The 5% vs 10% per month conflict is unresolved, and the current fixed fine for failing to register was not found.

## 9. Social security (NSSF) for a self-employed freelancer

### Takeaway
The NSSF is built around employers and employees. A self-employed freelancer has no compulsory NSSF contributions for their own activity. A voluntary sickness and maternity scheme for liberal professions and the self-employed was created by a 2002 decree, but the NSSF has reportedly suspended new enrolments. A newer pension law includes self-employed Lebanese residents, who would join by voluntary application. Its implementation status as of November 2026 is not confirmed. Members of regulated professions must join their professional syndicate, which often provides cover, but software development is not a regulated syndicate profession.

### Cited Findings
- A 2002 decree created a voluntary section of the sickness and maternity branch for liberal professions and self-employed persons, among others, with a monthly contribution of 90,000 LBP (then USD 60) for the self-employed. The NSSF has since suspended enrolment of the categories eligible for the voluntary scheme. — [ILO, Extending Social Health Protection to the uncovered in Lebanon (c. 2020–21)](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@arabstates/@ro-beirut/documents/publication/wcms_780987.pdf)
- New pension system: self-employed Lebanese living in Lebanon are among the categories covered, and voluntary participants must apply to enrol. — [ILO, Q&A on the new pension scheme / NSSF](https://www.ilo.org/media/480606/download) (about 2024; implementation status unconfirmed)
- The classic NSSF system is built on the employer–employee relationship and does not offer automatic individual self-employed membership. — [Furrsati blog, "NSSF social security for freelancers in Lebanon"](https://furrsati.com/blog/nssf-social-security-freelancers-lebanon) (low authority)
- Persons in liberal and other specialised professions must be members of the relevant professional syndicate. — [NRC, Lebanon Self-Employment and Small Business Guide](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/guidelines/guide-to-self-employment-and-small-business/lebanon-self-employment-and-small-business-guide_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- As of November 2026 the freelancer likely owes no compulsory NSSF contribution. Health cover would come from private insurance, or from the voluntary NSSF route if enrolment has reopened. If the developer incorporates and pays themselves a salary, or hires staff, NSSF employer registration and contributions follow (employer rules are not covered here).

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) NSSF statement was found on whether voluntary enrolment for the self-employed is open, at what contribution, or whether the new pension scheme has begun enrolling self-employed people. The pension law's number (believed to be Law No. 319/2023) was not verified in this research.

## 10. Sole proprietorship vs company (SARL/SAL) vs offshore company (Law 19/2008)

### Takeaway
A self-employed individual (or a sole proprietorship, مؤسسة فردية) pays the progressive scale of 4–25% after family deductions. A company (SARL or SAL) pays a flat 17% on profit plus 10% withholding on dividends, about 25.3% in total when everything is distributed, along with higher running costs. At typical indie-developer profit levels, staying an individual is cheaper (≈7% effective at $30k, ≈14% at $100k). An offshore company pays an annual lump-sum tax (reported at LBP 50 million after a 2022 amendment) instead of income tax, and its dividends are exempt. However, offshore companies are restricted in doing business in Lebanon, and no source confirms that a Lebanon-based developer could route app sales through one.

### Cited Findings
- Corporate income tax for resident companies is 17% of accounting profit after tax adjustments. — [PwC, Lebanon – Corporate – Taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)
- Dividend withholding tax is 10% of the distributed amounts. — [HBD-T/ALDIC, How to do business in Lebanon (2018)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/HOW-TO-DO-BUSINESS-IN-LEBANON-LEGAL-FISCAL-FRAMWORK-FOR-INVESTMENTS-updated-2018-Summary-presentation-ALDIC.pdf); 10% withholding on movable capital income generated in Lebanon — [PwC, Lebanon – Individual – Taxes on personal income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)
- Offshore companies pay an annual lump-sum tax of LBP 50 million (the same as holding companies; unified after the 2022 amendment). They are exempt from corporate income tax and from dividend withholding. Law No. 19/2008 widened their permitted activities. — [Hobeich Legal, Offshore company in Lebanon: formation and filings](https://hobeichlegal.com/guide/offshore-sal/); [Kallas Law Firm, Offshore companies in Lebanese law](https://www.klfi.net/blog-offshore-companies-lebanon-en/). Older sources give LBP 1 million (≈ USD 666), which is outdated — [Lexology, tax residence and fiscal domicile in Lebanon](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=43a019fe-9e7c-4908-b701-e06605fb19e6); [Wamda (2012)](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/en/2012/11/how-to-register-an-offshore-company-in-lebanon-legal-advice)
- No source was found showing that the 2024, 2025 or 2026 budgets changed the offshore lump-sum. — (absence of findings across the searches above; see Gaps)
- Sole proprietors and partnerships are taxed at progressive rates of 4–25%. — [Balance Brightness, "Income Tax Lebanon 2026"](https://balancebrightness.com/blog/income-tax-lebanon) (low authority; consistent with PwC and the MoF Citizen Budget)

### Inferences
- Comparison at USD 100k profit (single person, 89,500 rate):
  - Individual: ≈ USD 14.0k tax (section 3).
  - SARL distributing everything: 17% = USD 17.0k, plus 10% of the remaining 83k = USD 8.3k, so ≈ USD 25.3k.
  - A company only wins at very high profits, or where the profits are kept in the company.
- The offshore lump-sum (LBP 50m ≈ USD 559 at 89,500) looks attractive. But offshore companies are meant to do business outside Lebanon, and earning profit from activity physically carried out in Lebanon (the developer coding in Lebanon) sits awkwardly with the territorial rule in section 4 and with the offshore restrictions. A lawyer must confirm whether, under Law 19/2008, an offshore company with its developer in Lebanon can sell apps to foreign users, and how the developer's own pay (taxed as salary) and NSSF would then work.

### Gaps
- The precise list of activities Law 19/2008 allows offshore companies (e.g. whether "services to non-residents performed from Lebanon" or "software licensing" is allowed) was not obtained. Nor was any minimum capital or staffing rule.
- The current offshore lump-sum amount after the 2024–2026 budgets is not confirmed by a primary text.
- Company formation and running costs (lawyer, auditor, Commercial Register) were not researched.

## 11. Dividends, distributions and stamp duty relevant here

### Takeaway
As an individual there is no distribution tax. Profit is taxed once on the progressive scale. Through a SARL or SAL, dividends bear 10% withholding (exempt for offshore companies). Foreign investment income of the resident is taxed at 10% and paid in foreign currency. Stamp duty applies to contracts and some documents under Lebanese law. App-store sales concluded online with foreign platforms are unlikely to be stamped, but no source addressed this.

### Cited Findings
- Dividend withholding: 10% on distributed amounts; offshore company dividends are not subject to it. — [HBD-T/ALDIC (2018)](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/HOW-TO-DO-BUSINESS-IN-LEBANON-LEGAL-FISCAL-FRAMWORK-FOR-INVESTMENTS-updated-2018-Summary-presentation-ALDIC.pdf); [Hobeich Legal](https://hobeichlegal.com/guide/offshore-sal/)
- 10% tax on residents' income from foreign shares and bonds, payable in foreign currency since Nov 2022. — [Lloyds Bank Trade](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/lebanon/taxes); [PwC, Doing Business in Lebanon 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbilb.pdf)
- The 2026 budget law also changed stamp duty, VAT and inheritance tax; details were not obtained. — [Orbitax, Lebanon Budget 2026 measures](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851)

### Inferences
- For an individual publisher, stamp duty matters mainly for signed contracts with Lebanese parties (an office lease, a Lebanese contractor). Accepting the Apple or Google online agreements is unlikely to be treated as a stampable Lebanese document. This is an inference; no source was found.

### Gaps
- Current stamp duty rates (proportional and fixed) after Laws 324/2024 and 40/2026 were not confirmed from a citable source. A search summary mentioned fixed duties of LL 100,000 (contracts without a stated amount) and LL 20,000 (unpaid invoices) under the 2024 budget, but the underlying source could not be pinned, so it is not relied on.
- I could not check whether the 2026 budget's "exceptional" 17% tax on Sayrafa profits above USD 100k could affect an individual. It appears limited to past Sayrafa transactions, so it is likely irrelevant.
