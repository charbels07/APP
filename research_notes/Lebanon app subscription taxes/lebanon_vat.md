# Lebanese VAT (TVA / الضريبة على القيمة المضافة) for a Lebanon-resident freelance app developer selling subscriptions through the App Store and Google Play (rules as of November 2026)

> **Method and reliability note (read first).** Research date: 15 November 2026. Every finding below came from web-search result summaries and snippets. Full-page fetches were blocked in this session: `www.aldic.net` and `taxsummaries.pwc.com` did not resolve, so per session rules no other hosts were fetched. Those hosts must be allowed in the session's network settings before anyone can read the pages in full. Snippets can be taken out of context, so each point is cited to the page the search engine attributed it to. Those pages were not read end to end. Primary texts (Law 379/2001 as amended, Law 324/2024, Law 40/2026, MoF decisions) were **not** read in full. Points marked "secondary" or "uncertain" should be checked against the Official Gazette, the MoF site or a Lebanese tax adviser before anyone relies on them.
>
> Numbering note: the 2024 budget is Law No. 324/2024 and the 2026 budget is Law No. 40/2026 (published 10 Feb 2026). The 2025 budget was not voted by Parliament. Cabinet issued it by decree, and no VAT change in it was found.

## 1. Registration threshold (mandatory and voluntary) and the 2024 change

### Takeaway
Since Budget Law 324/2024 (Art. 14, amending VAT Law 379/2001), VAT registration is mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds **LBP 5 billion over one to four consecutive previous quarters**. The old threshold was LBP 100 million. Voluntary ("optional") registration is possible from **LBP 1 billion over 4 consecutive quarters**. **Importers and exporters must register whatever their turnover.** That exporter rule is the critical open question for a developer whose sales are exports of services. No source found shows that Law 40/2026 changed the thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Budget Law 324/2024 raised the mandatory registration threshold from LBP 100 million to **LBP 5 billion over a period of one to four consecutive quarters** — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Lebanon – Corporate – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/other-taxes) (search summary; the page also mentions the 2026 Budget Law, so it was updated in 2026); same figure on [PwC – Individual – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/other-taxes) (search summary says last reviewed July 2024).
- **Art. 14 of Law 324/2024** amends Law 379/2001 to set the compulsory threshold at LBP 5 billion over 4 consecutive quarters. The **optional** threshold was raised to **LBP 1 billion over 4 consecutive quarters** — [ALDIC, Summary of Law 324 (Budget 2024) tax amendments, June 2024](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summary-of-the-Law-324-Budget-2024-Tax-amendments.pdf) (search summary). The MoF "Citizen Budget 2024" also cites Art. 14 (per the same search summary; the document was not retrieved).
- **Transition:** businesses whose turnover was between LBP 100 million and LBP 5 billion in any year from 2020 to 2023 no longer have to register, and those already registered may ask to deregister — [ALDIC summary of Law 324/2024](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summary-of-the-Law-324-Budget-2024-Tax-amendments.pdf) (search summary).
- **Importers and exporters must register for VAT irrespective of turnover** — [PwC – Individual – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/other-taxes); also reported in the search summary of [ALDIC Law 324 summary](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summary-of-the-Law-324-Budget-2024-Tax-amendments.pdf).
- Law 324/2024 is dated 15 February 2024 and was published in Official Gazette No. 7 — [PwC – Corporate – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/other-taxes) (search summary).
- The MoF says offshore companies become subject to VAT when, among other conditions, they **export services from Lebanon abroad with the right of deduction**. The MoF therefore treats the export of services as an activity inside the VAT system (zero-rated), not one outside it — [MoF, التسجيل في الضريبة (VAT registration, companies)](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Companies/VAT/Pages/Registration-in-VAT.aspx) (search summary).
- A September 2025 article on the **2026 budget proposal** has a headline saying it raises "the VAT registration threshold for goods and services". This describes the draft, and the enacted text was not confirmed — [vatupdate, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/09/23/lebanons-2026-budget-proposal-vat-deduction-limits-and-expanded-digital-enforcement-measures/). A 2026 secondary guide still gives LBP 5 billion "set by the 2024 Budget Law" — [Kintsugi, Lebanon VAT Guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon).
- **Conflicting or outdated sources:** [Moore Global Lebanon Tax Guide (last modified 10/01/2025)](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/) gives LBP 500 million mandatory and LBP 100 million optional (the pre-2024 structure). [TaxAtlas](https://taxatlas.io/country/lebanon/vat-sales-tax) gives LBP 100 million. Both appear to predate Law 324/2024 and should not be relied on.

### Inferences
- **Converted at LBP 89,500/USD** (the rate Banque du Liban has used since 2024; this research did not source it): LBP 5 bn ≈ **USD 55,900**, LBP 1 bn ≈ USD 11,200, the old LBP 100 m ≈ USD 1,100. A solo developer's turnover could cross USD 55,900 within one to four quarters. It is unclear whether turnover means gross subscription prices or net proceeds from Apple/Google (see §4).
- Exports appear to count as taxable (zero-rated) turnover and not as exempt supplies, so they would probably count toward the LBP 5 bn threshold. This is not confirmed by a primary source.
- **Key ambiguity:** if "exporters" in the register-regardless-of-turnover rule includes exporters of *services* and not only of goods, a developer whose income is entirely exports of services could have to register from the first sale. The MoF's framing of offshore companies' "export of services" as a VAT-registrable activity is consistent with a broad reading. Practitioners commonly read "importers/exporters" as customs (goods) operators, but no source confirms that reading. Treat this as an open question for the MoF or an adviser.
- Voluntary registration (from LBP 1 bn) matters mainly if the developer has significant Lebanese input VAT (equipment, software, rent) to recover, since exports carry no output VAT. See the refund floor in §6.

### Gaps
- The amended text of the registration article of Law 379/2001 (as amended by Art. 14 of Law 324/2024) was not read. In particular, whether "exporters" covers services is unknown.
- Whether Law 40/2026 (2026 budget) changed the thresholds: the draft headline says it raised them, and no source shows what was enacted.
- Whether the threshold counts gross consumer prices or the developer's net proceeds is unknown.
- No MoF FAQ was found on registration for individual freelancers or service exporters.

## 2. Standard VAT rate and changes up to November 2026

### Takeaway
The standard rate is **11%**. In February 2026 Cabinet approved a rise to **12%** to fund public-sector pay. A VAT rate change needs a law, and no source found shows Parliament enacting it (it was still pending in July 2026). Treat 11% as in force but check the Official Gazette.

### Cited Findings
- Standard VAT rate 11%; unless specifically exempt, VAT is levied on all commercial transactions by business entities — [PwC – Corporate – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/other-taxes); [PwC Doing Business in Lebanon 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbilb.pdf). An older PwC guide says 10% (the pre-2018 rate) — [PwC Doing Business guide (older)](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guide-lebanon.pdf).
- Cabinet approved raising VAT from 11% to 12%, together with higher fuel charges, in mid-February 2026 — [Lexis Middle East, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-02-17_23/en); [The National, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/02/17/anger-in-lebanon-as-government-raises-vat-and-petrol-prices/); [vatupdate, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/02/18/lebanon-raises-vat-and-petrol-prices-to-fund-public-sector-salary-increases/); [Beirut.com](https://www.beirut.com/en/770834/12-vat-new-fuel-taxes-what-the-lebanese-govt-just-approved/); [Al Jazeera, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/2/23/lebanons-tax-hikes-draw-anger-from-economically-frustrated-public).
- The package was meant to raise about USD 200 million a year for public-sector wages. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber said the VAT change needs legislation, while the fuel increase took effect at once — [vatupdate, 26 Feb 2026 "pending parliamentary approval"](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/02/26/lebanon-plans-vat-hike-from-11-to-12-pending-parliamentary-approval/) (search summary).
- The rise to 12% was "not yet in force" and not enacted as of July 2026 — [Kintsugi, Lebanon VAT Guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon) (secondary).

### Inferences
- If a law raising the rate is passed, domestic supplies (for example any Lebanese-subscriber portion treated as domestic, §4) would move to 12%. Zero-rated exports stay at 0%.

### Gaps
- Whether Parliament voted the 12% rate between August and November 2026 was not found. No source after July 2026 confirms the status either way.

## 3. Place of supply and the export of services ("exempt with right of deduction")

### Takeaway
Lebanese VAT taxes goods and services **consumed in Lebanon**. Exports of services are "exempt with right of deduction", which the MoF itself equates with **zero-rating**: no output VAT, but input VAT is recoverable. The exporter must be registered and must **prove the export**. Secondary sources describe the conditions as a **non-resident recipient** and a service **used and enjoyed outside Lebanon**. Services linked to Lebanese assets, goods or establishments do not qualify. Since 2013 the MoF has not treated recharges to foreign entities as exports. The statutory wording was not retrieved, and secondary sources disagree on the article numbers.

### Cited Findings
- VAT applies from 1 February 2002 to all goods and services **consumed inside Lebanese territory** — [MoF, VAT (individuals) page](https://finance.gov.lb/AR-LB/TAXATION/INDIVIDUALS/VAT/Pages/default.aspx) (search summary).
- The MoF defines "exempt with right of deduction" operations as taxed **at a 0% rate**: goods or services supplied with no VAT charged, but the supplier may deduct input VAT. Its examples are **export** and international transport of persons and goods — [MoF, Levying the VAT / العمليات المعفاة](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Levying-the-VAT.aspx) (search summary).
- Secondary Arabic sources place these operations in **Arts. 19–21** of Law 379/2001: export of services, similar operations, international transport, and the commission of an agent acting in the name and for the account of the principal — [Tamima Ahmad al-Hassan, "VAT in Lebanon", jilrc.com](https://jilrc.com/archives/6469); [mohamah.net legal study](https://www.mohamah.net/law/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B6%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B6%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%AD/). **Conflict:** another search summary cited "Article 30" for the exemption of exports of services and of operations outside Lebanese territory. That attribution was unverified. Art. 59 is **not** the export article: it covers refunds for certain exempt-without-deduction sectors such as hospitals, education, medicines and non-profits (MoF page above; [IMF Country Report 23/8, 2023](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2023/english/1lbnea2023001.pdf)).
- The Lebanese University legal database (Law 379/2001 text) says export operations are exempt, the exporter has the right to deduct input VAT, and **an exporter exercising deduction must prove the export of goods or services** — [legallaw.ul.edu.lb, Law 379/2001](http://www.legallaw.ul.edu.lb/Law.aspx?lawId=203155) (search summary; the text was not read in full). To deduct, the person must be registered and hold supporting documents, including the export document — [taxesinlebanon (older explanatory site)](https://taxesinlebanon.tripod.com/VAT/WhyVAT.htm).
- **Conditions (secondary, practitioner guide):** a service to a non-resident can be zero-rated if the **recipient is non-resident** and the service is **used and enjoyed outside Lebanon**. Services relating to Lebanese real estate or goods, or supplied for a Lebanese establishment, are excluded, and services linked to Lebanese operations, assets or personnel are typically taxed at 11%. The supplier should keep evidence of the customer's non-resident status and of use abroad — [Umbrex, "How to hire an independent consultant in Lebanon"](https://umbrex.com/resources/how-to-hire-an-independent-consultant-in-any-country/how-to-hire-an-independent-consultant-in-lebanon/) (low-authority secondary source).
- **MoF narrow reading:** since 2013, recharges of expenses from a Lebanese entity to an entity abroad are **not treated as exports** and are taxed at the standard rate — [PwC – Corporate – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/other-taxes); [PwC Doing Business guide](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guide-lebanon.pdf).
- Exports of goods and services, export-related services, international transport and some intermediate operations are zero-rated — [PwC Doing Business guide](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guide-lebanon.pdf).
- The 2001 English translation of the VAT law refers to persons performing "exempted transactions that allow the right of deduction". It also obliges a Lebanese resident who uses a service acquired from abroad to declare and pay the tax — [VAT Law, December 2001, English translation (unofficial)](https://eduart0.tripod.com/sitebuildercontent/sitebuilderfiles/vatlaw_e02.pdf) (search summary).

### Inferences
- The test appears to look at both where the **customer is established** (non-resident) and where the service is **used** (outside Lebanon). For an app, "use" is wherever the subscriber's device is. Supplies to non-residents for use abroad should qualify, and use in Lebanon is the weak point (§4).
- The usual Lebanese proof for service exports is a contract or invoice to a foreign party plus payment from abroad, ideally in foreign currency through a bank. No primary source in this research states these conditions. Keep Apple/Google agreements, monthly financial reports and bank credits as proof.

### Gaps
- The exact wording and article number of the export-of-services provision, and of any place-of-supply rule for services, were not retrieved (Arts. 19–21 vs "Art. 30" conflict).
- No implementing decree or MoF instruction listing proof requirements for service exports (foreign-currency payment, customer certificates, etc.) was found. Decree 7308/2002 was not retrieved.

## 4. How App Store / Google Play subscription income is characterised, and the Lebanese-subscriber portion

### Takeaway
No MoF ruling, circular or guidance on App Store or Google Play income was found. The analysis therefore rests on general principles and is **genuinely ambiguous**. If the developer's supply is treated as made **to Apple/Google** (non-resident companies that resell, or act as commissionaires in their own name), the whole income looks like an export of services (zero-rated). If Apple/Google are treated as mere agents and the developer supplies each end user, subscribers located **in Lebanon** would be domestic supplies, and a registered developer would owe 11% on them. No source shows that Apple or Google collect Lebanese VAT.

### Cited Findings
- Lebanese law lists "the commission of an agent who intermediates in the name and for the account of his principal" among exempt-with-deduction operations. This shows the law distinguishes agents acting in the principal's name — [jilrc.com study](https://jilrc.com/archives/6469); [mohamah.net](https://www.mohamah.net/law/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B6%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B6%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%AD/) (search summaries).
- Recipient must be non-resident and the service used and enjoyed outside Lebanon for zero-rating — [Umbrex guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/how-to-hire-an-independent-consultant-in-any-country/how-to-hire-an-independent-consultant-in-lebanon/) (secondary).
- VAT applies to services consumed inside Lebanon — [MoF VAT page](https://finance.gov.lb/AR-LB/TAXATION/INDIVIDUALS/VAT/Pages/default.aspx).
- Apple administers tax for developers in more than 70 countries and regions and publishes its coverage list as an exhibit to the Developer Program License Agreement — [appsops.store blog](https://appsops.store/blog/app-store-tax-vat-gst-territories); [afternoon.co guide](https://www.afternoon.co/blog/apple-app-store-sales-tax-guide). An Apple agreement country list reproduced on manuals.plus shows "Lebanon" **without** the asterisk that marks countries such as Ireland*, Israel*, Italy*, Kazakhstan*, Latvia* — [manuals.plus copy of an Apple agreement list](https://manuals.plus/m/265e2c0212c971e429d1dc2a7816fc88b0efa2ad1cc77f2f307503ec7095d7ed). What the asterisk means and the document's date are unclear. **Uncertain.**
- Google Play help pages listing countries where Google handles VAT, as seen in search results, did not mention Lebanon — [Google Play Console Help, answer 138000](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000). **Not confirmed either way.**

### Inferences
- **Apple:** under Schedule 2 of Apple's agreement, Apple acts as agent in some territories and as commissionaire (selling in its own name) in others. This is general knowledge and not sourced here; the Apple/Google researcher covers it. Lebanon is probably not a territory where Apple collects VAT, which is consistent with the missing asterisk. In a commissionaire or reseller reading, the developer's taxable person is Apple (Apple Distribution International, Ireland) or Google (Google Commerce Ltd, Ireland). Both are non-residents, and the developer is paid from abroad in USD. That gives the strongest export position: the developer invoices or reports one non-resident customer, and its turnover is Apple/Google's net proceeds.
- **Lebanese subscribers:** in an agency reading, the subscription is used in Lebanon by a Lebanese resident, which fails the "used outside Lebanon" condition. A registered developer would arguably owe 11% on the Lebanese portion, computed on the consumer price including Apple/Google's commission, possibly VAT-inclusive. Under a strict agency analysis, the commission could in turn be an imported service subject to reverse charge (Art. 40, §5). This is a theoretical risk with no ruling found. In practice the Lebanese-subscriber share is often small, and Apple/Google sales reports give sales by country, so it can be quantified.
- The MoF's narrow stance on recharges to foreign entities (§3) suggests it reads "export" strictly. A conservative position is to treat the Lebanese portion as domestic, if registered. A defensible position is to treat all supplies as made to Apple/Google (non-resident) and so as exports. Ask an adviser or request an MoF ruling.
- If the developer is **below the LBP 5 bn threshold and not an "exporter" required to register** (§1), none of this creates a VAT liability. A non-registered person charges no VAT and must not issue VAT invoices (§7).

### Gaps
- No MoF circular, ruling or practice note on App Store / Google Play income, app subscriptions or online platforms was found (§8).
- The current Apple Schedule 2 exhibits and the Google Play tax table were not read directly, so whether Lebanon is a tax-collection territory is unconfirmed (another researcher covers this).

## 5. VAT on digital services from foreign providers (reverse charge, platform collection, digital services tax)

### Takeaway
Lebanon has **no special regime** for foreign digital sellers and **no digital services tax** in force. A non-resident supplying services must have a representative in Lebanon. Otherwise the **Lebanese resident beneficiary self-accounts** the VAT (Art. 40) in the periodic return or a special return, which in practice applies to businesses. In September 2026 the Finance Minister said the MoF is working on taxing foreign digital services consumed in Lebanon (streaming, cloud, **paid apps**, online ads). No draft or law had been published as of the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 40 of Law 379/2001:** for services from abroad to a resident of Lebanon, no agent needs to be appointed. The **resident beneficiary pays the VAT** in their periodic return or through a special return — [MoF, التصريح عن الضريبة (Declaration)](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-LB/taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Declaration.aspx) (search summary).
- If a Lebanese resident deals with a non-resident who owes VAT, the resident must check that the non-resident has a representative in Lebanon. Otherwise the resident must pay the tax and the fines. Services provided by a person resident outside Lebanon oblige the agent appointed in Lebanon to collect the tax — [MoF, Special cases / حالات خاصة](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Special-Cases.aspx) (search summary).
- Cross-border services such as SaaS from a foreign provider to a Lebanese business are generally self-accounted by the customer at 11% under a reverse charge. Lebanon has published no dedicated simplified regime for non-resident digital sellers — [Kintsugi, Lebanon VAT Guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon) (secondary).
- **Announced, not enacted:** on 20 September 2026, al-Marsad reported that the state is preparing to tax foreign digital services used by Lebanese people. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber said the ministry is working on subjecting them to tax. Possible scope: music and streaming, cloud storage, **paid apps**, online advertising (Google/Meta ads). The draft article, collection mechanism and scope had not been published — [almarsadonline, ~20 Sep 2026](https://almarsadonline.com/260178).
- The 2026 budget **proposal** included "expanded digital enforcement measures" (headline only) — [vatupdate, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/09/23/lebanons-2026-budget-proposal-vat-deduction-limits-and-expanded-digital-enforcement-measures/).
- Non-resident income: payers to non-residents must withhold and declare the tax (income-tax withholding, not VAT) — [MoF, Tax on Non-Residents](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Individuals/D-S/Pages/Tax-on-Non-Residents.aspx).

### Inferences
- There is currently no mechanism that makes Apple or Google collect Lebanese VAT from Lebanese consumers. This is consistent with Lebanon apparently not being an Apple tax-collection territory (§4). Lebanese consumers therefore pay no VAT on app subscriptions in practice.
- If a foreign digital services VAT is enacted in 2026–27, Apple/Google would probably have to register or collect on sales to Lebanese consumers. That would largely settle the Lebanese-subscriber question for the developer, with the platform as the deemed supplier. Watch the 2027 budget and any MoF draft.
- **Art. 40 matters for the developer's own purchases:** a VAT-registered developer who buys foreign services (Apple Developer Program fee, cloud hosting, SaaS tools, and possibly Apple/Google's commission under an agency reading) must self-account 11% on them and can normally deduct it in the same return.

### Gaps
- No primary text of a digital services tax or foreign-platform VAT rule was found. Its status as of mid-November 2026 is "announced/under study".
- Whether Art. 40 self-accounting formally applies to non-registered individuals (consumers) is unclear. The MoF wording says "resident beneficiary", and no enforcement against consumers was found.

## 6. Obligations once registered: returns, deadlines, forms, e-filing, invoicing, refunds

### Takeaway
VAT returns are **quarterly**. Since **Budget Law 40/2026** (published 10 Feb 2026, in force on publication), the periodic return **and payment are due within one month** after the quarter ends, instead of 20 days. Other 2026 changes: a **minimum refund claim of LBP 200 million**, input VAT on passenger cars capped at a USD 30,000 car value, and a 1.5% customs deposit for non-filers. The MoF extended VAT filing and payment, and a fine-settlement scheme, to **30 November 2026**. Details on the e-filing portal and invoice content were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Law 40/2026 (2026 budget), Art. 51:** the periodic VAT return deadline (Art. 35(4) of the VAT law) and the payment deadline (Art. 39) become **one month after the end of each quarter, instead of 20 days** — [PwC – Individual/Corporate – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/other-taxes) (search summary, July 2026); [ALDIC, Budget Law 2026 tax amendments](https://www.aldic.net/budget-law-2026-tax-amendments/); [ALDIC PDF summary of Law 40 (Budget 2026), March 2026](https://www.aldic.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Summary-of-the-Law-40-Budget-2026-Tax-amendments.pdf); [Kintsugi 2026 guide](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon).
- Law 40/2026 was published in the Official Gazette, **Issue 7, on 10 February 2026**, and took effect on publication. Several fixed lump-sum and minimum fines under the Tax Procedures Law were **raised 25-fold**. Input VAT on touring (passenger) cars is deductible only up to the VAT on a car worth **USD 30,000** before VAT, with exceptions — [Phoenix Law Firm, "Lebanon's 2026 Budget Law: tax, fee and fine changes"](https://phoenix-lawfirm.com/lebanon-2026-budget-law-tax-fee-fine-changes/) (search summary).
- **Minimum claimable VAT refund: LBP 200,000,000** (2026 rule, per search summary of the Law 40 sources) — [ALDIC Budget Law 2026](https://www.aldic.net/budget-law-2026-tax-amendments/); [Kintsugi 2026 guide](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon). Exactly which source states this was not verified.
- Customs may collect a **1.5% deposit** on each import from a taxpayer who has not filed business-results tax returns or VAT returns for any of the three preceding years — [Orbitax, Lebanon Budget 2026 measures](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851); [BDO Lebanon](https://www.bdo-lb.com/en-gb/insights/how-will-lebanon-s-rail-revival-affect-import-tax-compliance-for-logistics-businesses).
- **Before 2026:** VAT was due by the 20th of the month after the quarter — [Moore Global guide](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/). The Finance Minister has extended deadlines by decision, for example for the Q1 and Q4 2025 filings — [Lexis Middle East, 22 May 2025](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2025-05-22_25).
- **Current extension:** the MoF extended the deadline for filing and paying VAT, and the fine-settlement scheme, **until 30 November 2026** — [regfollower, "MoF issues three decisions on tax deadlines, VAT fines" (Nov 2026)](https://regfollower.com/lebanon-mof-issues-three-decisions-on-tax-deadlines-vat-fines/). **Decision 642/1** cuts VAT and salary-tax verification and collection penalties (50–60%) on assessments issued from 16 Nov 2022, until 30 Dec 2026. It gives **no** reduction for penalties on VAT self-assessments — [regfollower](https://regfollower.com/lebanon-grants-tax-penalty-reductions-on-eligible-assessments-until-end-2026/). An ALDIC listing dates related decisions to 18 Sep 2026 with a 31 Oct 2026 end date, so the dates **conflict**.
- **Forms:** the MoF publishes a guide to the **periodic VAT return (form ق1-2)** — [MoF guide, دليل التصريح الدوري (ق1-2)](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Taxation/Na/VAT/Tifsir/%D9%821-2%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD%20%D8%AF%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A.pdf). It also publishes a **special return for the period of late registration (form ق6-2)** — [MoF guide, ق6-2](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Taxation/Na/VAT/Tifsir/%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84%20%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD%20(%D9%826-2).pdf). Their contents were not read.
- **Refunds:** the MoF has a "Refunding VAT" page — [MoF, Refunding VAT](https://finance.gov.lb/en-us/Taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Refunding-VAT.aspx) (not read). Persons making exempt-with-deduction supplies (exports) may deduct input VAT (MoF "Levying the VAT" page, §3).
- **Invoicing:** no general e-invoicing mandate is in force. Electronic reporting has expanded for stamp-duty documents — [Kintsugi 2026 guide](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon) (secondary).

### Inferences
- For a quarter ending 31 December 2026, the return and payment would be due by about 31 January 2027 under the new one-month rule, subject to MoF extensions.
- A pure exporter usually has excess input VAT. With a **LBP 200 m (≈ USD 2,200) minimum refund claim**, small credits would be carried forward, not refunded. This lowers the value of voluntary registration for a small developer.
- Returns are filed in LBP. USD proceeds would have to be converted at the rate the MoF prescribes (not confirmed in this research).

### Gaps
- E-filing: the current MoF e-tax portal process, and whether individuals must e-file VAT returns, were not confirmed.
- Invoice content requirements (VAT number, Arabic language, LBP amounts, sequential numbering) for registered persons were not retrieved. Nor was whether a zero-rated export invoice to Apple/Google must mention the exemption.
- Refund procedure and timing for exporters (audit before refund, credit carry-forward rules) were not retrieved.

## 7. Penalties for failing to register (and related penalties)

### Takeaway
The MoF fines table lists a fixed fine for late or missing registration that depends on the type of taxpayer: **LBP 300,000 for individuals** in the historic figures. That table shows pre-2024 amounts, and the 2024 and 2026 budgets multiplied fixed fines (Law 40/2026: ×25 for several fixed and minimum fines). A taxpayer who registers late also has to file a special return for the late period (form ق6-2) and pay the tax due for it with late penalties. Charging VAT without being registered costs **three times the VAT shown on the invoice**. Late-filing and late-payment rates are reported inconsistently.

### Cited Findings
- **MoF fines table (VAT):** late or missing **registration application** costs about LBP 2 million for joint-stock companies, LBP 1 million for partnerships, LLCs and similar, and **LBP 300,000 for individuals and other taxpayers**. A non-registered person who issues an invoice showing VAT is fined **three times the VAT on the invoice**. Offenders remain subject to general laws where these do not conflict with the VAT law — [MoF, Fines – VAT (الغرامات – الضريبة على القيمة المضافة)](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Companies/FinesOff/Pages/VAT.aspx) (search summary; the table is garbled in places and may show outdated amounts).
- A 2019 academic paper describes a failure-to-register fine of **LBP 1 million plus all arrears (amounts and interest)** — [sapub.org, 2019](http://article.sapub.org/10.5923.j.mm.20190902.02.html). This is a pre-devaluation figure.
- Law 40/2026 raised several fixed lump-sum and minimum fines under the Tax Procedures Law **25-fold** — [Phoenix Law Firm](https://phoenix-lawfirm.com/lebanon-2026-budget-law-tax-fee-fine-changes/).
- There is a special return covering the period of delay in registration — [MoF guide ق6-2](https://www.finance.gov.lb/en-us/Taxation/Na/VAT/Tifsir/%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84%20%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD%20(%D9%826-2).pdf).
- **Late filing and late payment, conflicting sources:** late filing at 5% per month capped at 100% — [Moore Global](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/) — versus "the 2024 Budget Law raised the penalty to 10% of the tax per month, capped at 100%" — [Kintsugi 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/lebanon). Late payment is variously given as 1%, 1.5% or 3% per month — same sources (search summary). Unregistered taxpayers may face back-dated assessment plus penalties — [openaccountants skill page (low reliability)](https://skills.lc/openaccountants/openaccountants/openaccountants-openaccountants-agent-skills-lebanon-vat-skill-md).
- Customs 1.5% deposit on imports for taxpayers who have not filed VAT or income returns for any of the preceding three years — [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Lebanon-Budget-2026-Measures-I-61851).

### Inferences
- The main financial risk of not registering is not the fixed registration fine. It is the **back-assessment of VAT** on any supplies judged domestic (the Lebanese-subscriber portion, §4) for the whole late period, plus late-filing and late-payment penalties. Zero-rated exports generate no tax, so the penalties based on tax due would be small if all income is accepted as exports.
- The current LBP amount of the registration fine cannot be stated reliably. It is the historic amount multiplied by the 2024 and 2026 budget coefficients, which were not verified.

### Gaps
- The current (post-2024 and post-2026) LBP amount of the failure-to-register fine and the article of Tax Procedures Law 44/2008 that sets it were not retrieved.
- The authoritative late-filing and late-payment rates after Law 324/2024 and Law 40/2026 are not settled (sources conflict).

## 8. MoF circulars or rulings on App Store / Google Play / online platforms / freelancers for foreign clients

### Takeaway
**No** MoF circular, decision, ruling or FAQ was found on VAT for Apple App Store or Google Play income, app subscriptions, online platforms, or freelancers working for foreign clients. The only related official material is general: the MoF export and zero-rating explanation, Art. 40 self-accounting for imported services, and the September 2026 ministerial statement on taxing foreign digital services.

### Cited Findings
- MoF general guidance on zero-rated (exempt-with-deduction) exports — [MoF, Levying the VAT](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Levying-the-VAT.aspx).
- MoF guidance that offshore companies exporting services from Lebanon abroad are within VAT with the right of deduction — [MoF, Registration in VAT](https://www.finance.gov.lb/ar-lb/Taxation/Companies/VAT/Pages/Registration-in-VAT.aspx).
- Art. 40 self-accounting by resident beneficiaries of services from abroad — [MoF, Declaration](https://finance.gov.lb/ar-LB/taxation/Individuals/VAT/Pages/Declaration.aspx).
- Ministerial statement (Sept 2026) that the MoF is working on taxing foreign digital services, including paid apps, with no published text — [almarsadonline](https://almarsadonline.com/260178).
- A Lebanese practitioner guide to income tax and tax procedures "according to texts in force up to the 2026 budget" exists, but its VAT or freelancer content was not retrieved — [Kallas Law Firm](https://www.klfi.net/lebanon-tax-guide-income-tax-procedures/).

### Inferences
- With no specific guidance, a developer who is (or may be) required to register would reduce risk by asking the MoF's VAT directorate in writing (or an adviser) two questions. (1) Does "exporters must register regardless of turnover" cover service exporters? (2) Are App Store / Google Play proceeds an export of services to the non-resident platform, including the share of Lebanese subscribers?

### Gaps
- MoF circulars and decisions from 2024–2026 could not be searched systematically: the MoF site was not fetched, and no index of VAT circulars was retrieved. A ruling on this point may exist without having been found.
- Income tax treatment of the same income (freelancer / non-commercial profession, withholding) is out of scope here and not covered.
