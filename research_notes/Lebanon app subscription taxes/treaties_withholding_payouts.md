## 1. US position: Is there a US–Lebanon income tax treaty? What does W-8BEN allow? Is the income US-source FDAP (royalty) or not? What do Apple and Google say?

### Takeaway
The United States has **no income tax treaty with Lebanon**. A Lebanese individual therefore cannot claim any treaty rate on Form W-8BEN (Part II stays blank), and any income the US treats as US-source FDAP income is withheld at the 30% statutory rate. But Apple's own forum statement, the commissionaire/agency structure, and the US sourcing rules all point the same way: ordinary App Store and Google Play **sales proceeds** paid to a non-US developer with no US office are generally **not** treated as US-source royalties, so they are normally not subject to US withholding. The developer still has to file a W-8BEN to prove foreign status. Apple's and Google's exact current withholding practice for a non-treaty country could not be confirmed from their help pages and must be checked in App Store Connect and Play Console.

### Cited Findings

- A 2026 schedule of US withholding by country lists **Lebanon at 30%** and attributes its rates to the IRS tax treaty table. 30% is the rate for non-treaty countries. — [AST, Foreign Country US Withholding Tax Schedule 2026 (May 2026)](https://www.ast.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Foreign-Country-US-Withholding-Tax-Schedule_2026.pdf) **[secondary, financial-services firm]**

I could not open either page to read the country list myself (irs.gov was blocked by the network). — [IRS, United States income tax treaties – A to Z](https://irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z); [IRS Table 3 – List of tax treaties (PDF)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-lbi/table-3-list-of-tax-treaties.pdf) **[official; not opened]**

### Inferences
- **W-8BEN for this developer:** the developer is a foreign individual, Lebanese resident, sole proprietor, so the form is the W-8BEN (individual), not the W-8BEN-E. The country of residence is Lebanon. **Part II (treaty claim) must be left blank**, because there is no US–Lebanon treaty. Without a treaty claim, a US TIN (SSN/ITIN) is not needed. A foreign TIN, such as the Lebanese MoF tax number, may be asked for. Renew it every 3 years or when circumstances change, for example a move or a new address.
- **Source characterisation (my reading of US law, not confirmed by an IRS page in this session):**
  - Under Treas. Reg. §1.861-18, the transfer of a "copyrighted article" (a software or app licence to an end user that does not carry the right to copy and distribute) is treated as a **sale of goods, not a royalty**.
  - Under the residence-of-seller rule for personal property (IRC §865), sales income of a non-resident individual with no US office is generally **foreign-source**. It is therefore **not FDAP and not subject to 30% withholding**, and it is not effectively connected income (ECI) when there is no US trade or business.
  - Subscriptions are recurring access, which could be analysed as a service or a "digital content transaction". The 2025 IRS rules on cloud and digital-content transactions could matter here; this was not verified.
  - This reading matches the Apple forum statement and the agency/commissionaire structure, in which Apple or Google sells to the end user and remits proceeds to the developer.
  - **Risk:** income the platform labels a "royalty", or income from US-sourced services (e.g. ads, sponsored work, consulting for US clients), could be treated differently.
- **Practical effect if a platform did withhold 30%:** with no treaty, the only route back would be a US nonresident return (Form 1040-NR) arguing the income is not US-source. Lebanon gives no unilateral credit (see Q2), so the 30% would be a pure cost.
- In-app **advertising** revenue (e.g. Google AdMob or AdSense) is a different stream. Google withholds US tax on the US-viewer share of some ad and creator revenue for non-US payees. Without a treaty this would be 30% for a Lebanese resident. This is from my own background knowledge, not verified here.

## 2. Lebanon's double tax treaties, and Lebanese domestic relief for foreign tax

### Takeaway
Lebanon reports about **34 signed treaties with 32 countries**, mostly Arab, Eastern European and Mediterranean countries. Confirmed or likely in force: France, Malta, Cyprus, Turkey, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Jordan, Egypt and others. There is **no treaty with the UK** (HMRC), with the **US**, or (per Spain's trade portal) with **Spain**. No source suggests a treaty with **Ireland** (Apple Distribution International), **Singapore**, or **Luxembourg**, but this could not be confirmed from an official Lebanese list. Domestically, Lebanon taxes on a **territorial** basis and has **no specific foreign tax credit rules**. Any foreign tax withheld on a Lebanon-based developer's earnings is therefore effectively unrecoverable in Lebanon, except possibly under a treaty with the paying country.

### Cited Findings
- **Count:** "Lebanon has signed 34 Conventions for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion on Taxes from Income and Capital with 32 countries" (a signed count, not an in-force list). — [IDAL / Invest in Lebanon – International agreements](https://investinlebanon.gov.lb/en/doing_business/legal_framework/international_agreements) **[official, Lebanese investment agency; date not verified]**
- **PwC partial list** of Lebanese treaties: Czech Republic, Egypt, France, Iran, Pakistan, Poland, Qatar, Romania, UAE, Ukraine, Yemen (PwC says the list is not complete). — [PwC Tax Summaries – Lebanon, Individual – Foreign tax relief and tax treaties](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties) **[secondary, Big-4; not opened]**
- **Malta:** Malta's foreign ministry lists the Lebanon convention as "In force", and a Lebanese order from 2000 gives it effect. — [Malta Ministry for Foreign Affairs – Lebanon DTA](https://affarijietbarranin.gov.mt/?p=21541) **[official, Malta]**
- **Cyprus:** the Cyprus Ministry of Finance double-taxation-agreements page came up for this query (Lebanon listed among Cyprus's DTAs). The entry itself was not read. — [Cyprus MoF – Double taxation agreements](https://mof.gov.cy/en/legislation/1-double-taxation-agreements) **[official, Cyprus; content not confirmed]**
- **Turkey:** an English text of the Turkey–Lebanon tax treaty is published by a Turkish adviser. — [CottGroup – lubnan-EN.pdf](https://www.cottgroup.com/images/ikili-anlasmalar/VERGI/lubnan-EN.pdf) **[secondary]**
- **United Kingdom: no treaty.** "There is no comprehensive double taxation agreement between the United Kingdom and Lebanon." — [HMRC Double Taxation Relief Manual DT11850 – Lebanon](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt11850) **[official, UK]**. **Contradiction:** an aggregator lists "the UK" among Lebanon's major treaty partners — [taxatlas.io – Lebanon](https://taxatlas.io/tax-treaties/lebanon) **[secondary; contradicted by HMRC, so treat it as wrong]**
- **Spain: no treaty.** Spain and Lebanon "are not bound by a double taxation agreement." — [ACCIÓ (Catalonia) trade portal – Lebanon taxes](https://tradeportal.accio.gencat.cat/ca/mercats-potencials/liban/impostos) **[official, regional government]**
- **Territoriality:** Lebanon's income tax law is Decree-Law 144 of 1959. Its territoriality rule (Article 3, per a policy paper) is "a very broad concept of territoriality without prejudice to Double Taxation Treaties (DTTs) signed by Lebanon and ratified by law." — [The Policy Initiative, Karim Daher, *Digital Technology* paper (12 Nov 2025)](https://api.thepolicyinitiative.org/content/uploads/files//KarimDaher_DigitalTechnology_12Nov2025.pdf) **[secondary, Lebanese think tank; tax lawyer author]**
- "Lebanon adopts a territorial tax system under which all income sourced in Lebanon is subject to tax in the country." PwC notes that resident companies are not taxed on foreign-source income from activities carried out abroad through foreign branches. — [PwC – Lebanon corporate income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/income-determination); [PwC – Lebanon taxes on corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income) **[secondary]**
- **No foreign tax credit:** PwC says "there are no specific regulations concerning foreign tax credit in Lebanon." Moore Global says there is no foreign tax relief. PwC also says taxes paid to foreign governments on income earned in Lebanon are **non-deductible**. — [PwC – Lebanon corporate tax credits and incentives](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/tax-credits-and-incentives); [Moore Global – Lebanon](https://www.moore-global.com/services/tax/international-corporate-tax/lebanon/) **[secondary]**
- The IMF (2023) recommended replacing the income tax law with a new one, so the framework may change. — [IMF Country Report 2023 (Lebanon)](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2023/english/1lbnea2023001.pdf.md) **[official, IMF]**

### Inferences
- **The paying entities' countries are probably all non-treaty countries for Lebanon:**
  - Apple pays non-US developers through entities such as Apple Distribution International (Ireland) and other regional Apple entities.
  - Google Play has historically paid through Google LLC (US), Google Commerce Ltd (Ireland), and Google Asia Pacific (Singapore).
  - The US is confirmed non-treaty. For Ireland, Singapore and Luxembourg, no source suggests a treaty. I believe none exists, but this is unverified.
  - **Treaty relief is therefore unlikely to be available** against any tax those countries might withhold. In practice, Ireland and Singapore do not normally withhold on these sales payouts.
- **Lebanese taxable base:** a developer who is resident and works in Lebanon is very likely taxed in Lebanon on app earnings as business or professional income (Decree-Law 144, Title I), even though the buyers are abroad. The activity (developing and publishing) is carried out in Lebanon, and the "broad territoriality" reading points that way. This is my reading, to be confirmed by the researcher covering Lebanese domestic tax.
- **Foreign tax withheld** (e.g. Brazilian WHT on Brazil sales, see Q3; or US tax if a platform withheld it) is then **neither creditable nor clearly deductible** in Lebanon. Whether it could be claimed as an ordinary business expense is untested. PwC's "non-deductible" statement concerns foreign taxes on income earned in Lebanon, which is exactly this case. The practical answer: keep foreign withholding at zero by filing the right forms and residency certificates where the platform offers them.
- Since it is territorial, Lebanon does not tax genuinely foreign-source business income. Moving the payout bank account abroad **does not change the source** of the income: the work is still done in Lebanon.

### Gaps
- No official Lebanese Ministry of Finance (finance.gov.lb) list of treaties in force was found in search, and the site could not be fetched. The full list of about 32 countries, and which treaties are ratified and in force, remains unverified.
- Not confirmed: Ireland, Singapore, Luxembourg, Netherlands (absence of a treaty). Not confirmed either: whether the Lebanon–France and Lebanon–Cyprus treaties are in force, or how their "royalties" and "business profits" articles would apply.
- The text of Decree-Law 144, Articles 1–3 (territoriality) and any provision on foreign tax deduction or credit, was not read directly.
- Not verified: Lebanese tax on foreign-source **investment** income (interest/dividends on a foreign bank account) for residents, under the movable-capital income tax. This matters if payouts sit in a foreign interest-bearing account.

## 3. Other countries' withholding on app-store developer earnings (short)

### Takeaway
The main withholding a Lebanon-based developer may meet is **Brazil**. Both Google and Apple deduct Brazilian withholding tax on Brazil-storefront sales unless a residency certificate reduces it, and Lebanon has no treaty with Brazil to rely on (to my knowledge, not verified). The other cases found mostly concern developers **located in** the country concerned:
- India WHT applies to India-located developers on Google Play.
- Japan's reverse charge and Australia's GST registration apply to Japan- or Australia-based developers.
Mexico withholding documents also exist on Apple.

### Cited Findings
- **Google, Brazil:** when you are paid in a non-BRL currency, "Google or its payment processor partners will deduct the full cost of Brazilian withholding taxes (WHT) from your earnings." Treaty rates may apply with a residency certificate; Japan is among the listed supported countries. — [Google Play Console Help 9384608 – Withholding tax](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608?hl=en); [Google Payments Center 13401799](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/13401799) **[official]**
- **Google, India:** "If you're located in India and offer Google Play Store app and in-app purchases, Google will deduct Indian withholding taxes (WHT) from your proceeds." Quarterly Form 16A certificates go to India-based merchants. This applies to India-located developers only. — [Google Play Console Help 9384608](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608) **[official]**
- **Apple, Brazil and Mexico:** if you don't provide a certificate, "your sales in Brazil may be subject to the applicable withholding tax at the standard rate". "Monthly withholding tax information for your sales on the App Store in Brazil and Mexico are available for download." — [App Store Connect Help – Manage invoices and other tax documents](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/manage-invoices-and-other-tax-documents) **[official]**
- **Apple, Japan and Australia:** the reverse charge for Japanese consumption tax applies to developers whose principal place of business is in Japan. Apple requires Australia-based developers to have a GST registration. — [App Store Connect Help – Understanding taxes](https://developer-mdn.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/making-payments-to-apple/understanding-taxes) (a mirror of developer.apple.com) **[official]**
- An unofficial site says India and Korea have historically had App Store withholding effects. — [appsops.store](https://appsops.store/blog/app-store-tax-vat-gst-territories) **[secondary, unverified]**

### Inferences
- For a Lebanese developer, Brazil WHT would come out of Brazil-storefront proceeds at the standard (non-treaty) rate. Under Q2 this is not creditable in Lebanon, so it is a straight cost on Brazilian sales only.
- Consumption taxes (VAT/GST/sales tax) on consumers are collected and remitted by Apple and Google in most storefronts. That is a separate subject covered by other researchers, and not a withholding on the developer.

### Gaps
- Exact Brazil WHT rates and whether Lebanon appears in Google's or Apple's residency-certificate lists were not seen. Indonesia, Mexico and other jurisdictions' rules for **non-resident** developers were not confirmed.

## 4. Lebanese banking in 2025–2026: fresh-dollar accounts, capital controls, bank resolution/gap law, banking secrecy, FATF and EU listings

### Takeaway
Lebanon has **no capital-controls law**. Banks have applied **informal** limits since October 2019, but **"fresh" deposits** (money arriving from abroad after 17 November 2019, cleared through BDL accounts under **Basic Circular 165** of April 2023) are kept apart from frozen pre-crisis deposits and **can generally be withdrawn and transferred abroad**. This makes a "fresh USD" account the workable local option.

On the restructuring laws:
- The 2025 **bank resolution law** was amended in **August 2026** but is effectively dormant until the **Financial Gap Law** is in force. The Gap Law is still contested, and the IMF is reviewing the changes as of October 2026.
- **Banking secrecy** was loosened again in **April 2025**.

On the listings:
- Lebanon has been on the **FATF grey list since October 2024**. It was **kept there in June 2026 and again at the 28–30 October 2026 plenary** (secondary reports).
- The **EU added Lebanon to its high-risk third-country list** by Delegated Regulation **2025/1184** (in force about 5 August 2025).

Expect **enhanced due diligence, document requests and possible delays** on incoming wires from Apple's or Google's EU or US entities, and on any foreign account a Lebanese resident tries to open.

### Cited Findings
- **Capital controls:** "While legally Lebanon is a free-market economy and does not restrict the movement of capital into or out of the country, Lebanese banks have imposed informal capital controls on dollar withdrawals and financial outflows since October 2019." No comprehensive capital-controls law has been enacted. Post-2019 deposits are treated separately from pre-2019 deposits and **may generally be transferred abroad**. — [US State Department, 2026 Investment Climate Statement – Lebanon (published September 2026)](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/lebanon); [PDF](https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/701264_2026-Lebanon-Investment-Climate-Statement.pdf) **[official, US government]**
- **BDL Basic Circular 165 (19 April 2023):**
  - Banks must open new LBP and USD accounts at BDL reserved for settling cheques and transfers in "fresh funds" (intermediate decision 13548).
  - **Fresh money** = funds transferred from abroad or foreign banknotes received after **17 November 2019**, plus cash deposited into new LBP accounts.
  - Fresh-money clearing began on **12 June 2023**.
  — [RegAlert – BDL decision 13548 (19 April 2023)](https://regalert.today/document/5e300c38-625a-4e58-849f-9d32b45deb57/text) **[secondary, regulatory database reproducing BDL text]**; [LBC – Circular 165 takes effect](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/news-bulletin-reports/706075/circular-165-takes-effect-lebanese-banks-urged-to-comply-with-fresh-cu/en) **[secondary, news]**
- **Criticism of Circular 165:** critics say it creates a two-tier system and warn that fresh funds pass through BDL clearing accounts and could become part of BDL's reserves. In 2025, lawyers petitioned the State Council to annul Circulars 151 and 165; no ruling was found. — [Majalla – "How an obscure bank circular will worsen Lebanon's financial crisis"](https://en.majalla.com/node/292111/business-economy/how-obscure-bank-circular-will-worsen-lebanons-financial-crisis) **[secondary, opinion]**; [LCPS – Central bank circulars and deposit access in 2025](https://www.lcps-lebanon.org/en/articles/details/5004/central-bank-circulars-and-deposit-access-in-2025) **[secondary, think tank]**
- **Old (pre-2019) deposits:** BDL intermediate decision **13771 (28 November 2025)** sets a USD 9,200 annual cap on transfers to "Special Sub-Accounts", with USD 500 monthly withdrawals, and limits bank secrecy on those sub-accounts to BDL and the Banking Control Commission. This concerns legacy deposits, not fresh ones. — [RegAlert – BDL decision 13771 (28 Nov 2025)](https://regalert.today/document/c4efb249-e7e6-46f4-a25b-d9fbfaa1681c/text) **[secondary, reproduces BDL text]**
- **Banking secrecy:** Parliament amended the banking secrecy law in 2022 and again in **2025**. This gave BDL, the Banking Control Commission and external auditors wider access to account data. — [LCPS 2025](https://www.lcps-lebanon.org/en/articles/details/5004/central-bank-circulars-and-deposit-access-in-2025) and search summary **[secondary]**.   - In August 2026, Parliament amended the 2025 bank resolution law. Article 29 and three other provisions now defer implementation to the Financial Regularity and Deposit Recovery Law (the "Gap Law"). The resolution law is described as "structurally dormant". — [Beirut Core – "Passed, and Still Suspended" (Aug 2026)](https://www.beirutcore.com/briefs/source/SF-LBN-LEGAL-2026-08) **[secondary]**

It was published on 16 July 2025, and the list took effect about **5 August 2025** (20 days after publication; one source says July). — [EUR-Lex – Delegated Regulation 2025/1184](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/1184/oj/eng); [European Commission news (16 July 2025)](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/news/protecting-eus-financial-system-2025-07-16_nl); [Rosemont (Aug 2025)](https://rosemont-int.com/article/news/aml-eu-list-of-high-risk-third-countries-august-2025) **[official + secondary]**
  - Later amendments: 2025/1393 (review clause) and 2026/46 and 2026/83 (adopted 3–4 December 2025). The list was reported at 26 countries from 29 January 2026, but the snippet did not show whether Lebanon is among them. — [EUR-Lex 2025/1393](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/1393/oj/eng/pdf); [Bird & Bird (2026)](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/eu-list-of-highrisk-countries-and-jurisdictions-for-money-laundering-and-terrorism-financing-amended) **[official + secondary]**
  - Practical effect reported: civil-society funding into Lebanon was affected by the EU listing (extra checks, delays). — [Hivos EU SEE alert](https://eusee.hivos.org/alert/civil-society-funding-affected-as-lebanon-responds-to-eu-high-risk-listing/) **[secondary, NGO]**

### Inferences
- **Receiving Apple/Google payouts in Lebanon:** an incoming USD wire from abroad credited to a **fresh USD account** falls under Circular 165. On the State Department's description, it should be withdrawable as cash and transferable abroad, unlike "lollar" pre-2019 deposits. Practical points:
  - Use only a **fresh** sub-account. Never let payouts land in a legacy account.
  - Expect bank fees (fresh-account, incoming-wire and cash-withdrawal commissions vary by bank).
  - Expect source-of-funds questions. Keep App Store Connect / Play Console financial reports and payout statements as proof.
- **EU and FATF listings and the platforms:** under the EU AML Directive (2015/849, Art. 18a; my knowledge, not fetched), EU obliged entities must apply **enhanced due diligence** to business relationships and transactions involving high-risk third countries. That covers EU banks, payment institutions, and Irish/Luxembourg payment flows from Apple or Google entities. Consequences:
  - Correspondent-bank delays or rejected wires to Lebanese banks are plausible.
  - Opening an EU (e.g. Cypriot or French) account as a Lebanese resident will involve EDD (source of funds, proof of activity) and may be refused.
  - None of this makes receiving payouts illegal. It is friction, not prohibition.
- **Depositor risk:** the Gap Law and the resolution framework mainly address pre-2019 losses. Whether a future bank resolution could touch fresh deposits is not settled in the sources. The BDL carve-out debate concerns post-October-2019 **capital**, not deposits. A cautious developer would not keep large balances in a Lebanese bank and would sweep funds out regularly.

### Gaps
- No primary BDL text of Circular 165 or its later amendments was read (bdl.gov.lb not reachable). Whether any 2025–2026 BDL circular changed fresh-account rules is unverified.
- The exact law number and date of the 2025 banking secrecy amendment, and the bank resolution law number, were not confirmed from the Official Gazette.
- The official FATF October 2026 text on Lebanon was not read. The "remains listed" status rests on consistent secondary reports.
- Not confirmed: whether Lebanon is still on the EU list after the January 2026 amendments. It is likely while it stays FATF grey-listed, but this is an inference.

## 5. Receiving payouts abroad or via payment services: legality, Wise/Payoneer availability, Apple/Google bank-country rules, Lebanese declaration duties

### Takeaway
Nothing found makes it illegal for a Lebanese resident to hold a foreign bank account or receive business income into it. Lebanon has no capital-controls law; State Department says capital movement is legally free. Foreign banks and payment institutions will apply enhanced checks to Lebanese residents (Q4).
- **Google Play** supports developer and merchant registration from **Lebanon**, with **LBP as the default currency** in Google's table. Whether USD payouts are available needs checking.
- **Apple** decides the payout set-up by the **bank branch's country**. Its help pages conflict on whether the account holder must be the enrolled developer.
- **Wise** can send USD to Lebanon by wire, but there is no evidence that Lebanese residents can open Wise accounts; the Wise card is reportedly unavailable to them.
- **Payoneer**'s status in Lebanon is **disputed** (reported blocked, with mixed user reports).
Lebanon is a CRS participant, so foreign accounts will be reported to the Lebanese tax authority. No specific individual foreign-account declaration form was found.

### Inferences
- **Practical options, ranked by robustness (my synthesis):**
  1. **A fresh USD account at a Lebanese bank**, in the developer's own name, set as the Apple/Google payout account. This is the simplest compliant route: name, address and country all match. Risks: Google paying in **LBP** (verify that USD is selectable), bank fees, EDD delays, and Lebanese bank risk. Sweep the balance out regularly.
  2. **A foreign account in the developer's own name**, e.g. Cyprus, UAE or France, where the developer can pass KYC. Apple accepts a bank in another country because the branch country drives processing. Whether Google allows a bank country different from the payments-profile country was not confirmed; historically Google requires the bank to be in the payments profile's country or to accept the profile currency. Expect EU banks to apply EDD (Q4). The UAE is not EU-bound but has its own checks.
  3. **Payment services:** Wise, as an account for Lebanese residents, is not shown to be available. Payoneer is disputed, so do not rely on it without confirming directly.
- Whichever account is used, the income stays **Lebanese-taxable** (Q2), and CRS means a foreign account will likely be reported to the Lebanese MoF. Declaring the income is the safe course.
- An account in someone else's name (a relative abroad) runs into Apple's and Google's holder rules and AML concerns, and should be avoided.

## 6. Sanctions: is Lebanon under comprehensive OFAC sanctions?

### Takeaway
No. OFAC's Lebanon-related measures are **targeted**: Hizballah and associated persons (SDGT under E.O. 13224), certain officials and networks, and money exchangers. Lebanon is not comprehensively embargoed, so Apple and Google payouts to a non-designated Lebanese individual are not blocked by sanctions. Banks' screening, and the secondary-sanctions risk for foreign banks under the Hizballah financing law, explain the extra compliance checks.

### Cited Findings
- Lebanon-related measures "focus on specific individuals, entities, and networks rather than the Lebanese state or economy as a whole". Ordinary trade and remittances with non-sanctioned Lebanese parties remain legal for US persons. — [Model Diplomat – OFAC Lebanon sanctions](https://modeldiplomat.com/learn/glossary/ofac-lebanon-sanctions) **[secondary, glossary]**
- Most Hizballah designations use **E.O. 13224** (SDGT). The Hizballah Financial Sanctions Regulations implement the Hizballah International Financing Prevention Act, under which foreign financial institutions that knowingly facilitate significant transactions for Hizballah can be sanctioned. — [Orrick InfoBytes (2022)](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2022-12-07/ofac-sanctions-hizballah-accountants-and-weapons-facilitator/); [Federal Register 2026-10432 (SDGT list notice)](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-10432.pdf) **[secondary + official]**
- **Recent actions:**
  - November 2025: Treasury targeted unlicensed money exchanges and exchange companies used to launder money through Lebanon's cash economy. — [Treasury press release via GlobalSecurity (6 Nov 2025)](https://www.globalsecurity.org/security/library/news/2025/11/sec-251106-treasury01.htm) **[official text via mirror]**
  - June 2026: Treasury designated Lebanese officials for obstructing the peace process and Hizballah's disarmament. — [Treasury bulletin (June 2026)](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USTREAS/bulletins/41cacdc) **[official]**

### Inferences
- A Lebanese developer should make sure the receiving bank or exchange is **not designated**. Avoid cash exchangers and unlicensed money transfer businesses for converting payouts, given the November 2025 actions. A licensed bank fresh account is the low-risk path.

### Gaps
- The OFAC sanctions programs list and Lebanon program pages (ofac.treasury.gov) were not opened directly. Not checked: whether a separate "Lebanon-Related Sanctions" programme (E.O. 13441) is still listed.

## 7. Exchange rate for tax and paying taxes in USD

### Takeaway
Since the 2024 unification, the **official BDL rate is LBP 89,500 per USD**. It is used for taxes and fees and for the **2026 budget**, and PwC applies it to convert foreign-currency salaries. A Lebanese developer would convert USD income at 89,500 when computing Lebanese tax, unless the MoF issues a different rule. **No 2026 Ministry of Finance rule on paying income tax in USD was found.**

### Cited Findings
- The rate has held at about LBP 89,500/USD since 2024, described as "formal rate unification at 89,500 LBP per dollar", both official and parallel. It "serves as the basis for calculating public expenditures, taxes, and fees." — [bne IntelliNews – Lebanon's exchange rate stabilisation mechanism](https://www.intellinews.com/lebanon-s-exchange-rate-stabilization-mechanism-how-it-works-and-what-it-has-achieved-449827/) **[secondary, news]**
- PwC: "the Central Bank's highest published official rate is currently LBP 89,500/USD 1", applied to foreign-currency salaries. — [PwC – Lebanon individual – Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/other-taxes) **[secondary, Big-4; the exact PwC page carrying the quote was not confirmed]**
- **2026 budget:** "its value in U.S. dollars was calculated based on an estimated exchange rate of USD 1 = LBP 89,500." — [Institut des Finances Basil Fuleihan (Ministry of Finance), Citizen Budget 2026 (April 2026)](https://institutdesfinances.gov.lb/sites/default/files/2026-04/Citizen%20Budget%202026-Apr26-En-final_0.pdf) **[official, MoF training institute]**
- **Older conflicting report:** the 15,000 rate was "still used by Lebanon's tax administration". This predates the 2024 unification and is likely outdated. — [L'Orient Today – Sayrafa and market rates align](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1361551/lebanese-lira-sayrafa-and-market-rates-align.html) **[secondary, outdated]**

### Inferences
- Income records should be kept in USD, converted at 89,500 for the Lebanese return. If Google pays in LBP (Q5), the LBP amounts are the starting point, but the conversion and banking risk then sits with the developer.

### Gaps
- No MoF circular found on whether income tax on USD earnings must or can be paid in USD (fresh) or in LBP. Earlier budget laws (2024) reportedly had provisions on fresh-dollar payment of some taxes, but this was not verified. finance.gov.lb was not reachable for the 2025/2026 budget law texts.
- Not checked: whether BDL changed the official rate after April 2026 (the latest dated source).
